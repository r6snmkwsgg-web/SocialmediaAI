# Decisions

Defaults picked while building, so nobody has to guess why something is the way it is. Change any of them; each says where it lives.

## Order of work

- The account system (Phase 2) was built before streamer settings and session stats (Phase 1, items 3 and 4), because saving settings "per account" needs accounts. Everything else in Phase 1 came first.

## Live

- **The vote** (`lib/live/show.ts`): it runs on the win screen, which is 10 seconds long when voting is on and 7 when it's off. One vote per viewer, and typing again changes it. A gift during the vote adds 1 vote per coin with no cap, so a Galaxy is 1,000 votes, the same as 1,000 Roses (it was capped at 50, which made a Galaxy worth 50 Roses); those votes stay with the gifter and count once they type A, B or C. Ties are broken at random. When nobody votes, the next round is picked at random and nothing is announced.
- **Every coin counts about the same** (`lib/live/games/battle.ts`, `worth` in `lib/live/menu.ts`): in the battle and Rivals a coin is about 1 HP, whatever the gift. A Rose heals 1 HP; a ball carries its gift's coins as health; rage and the shield heal 1 HP a coin on top of the power; a meteor gift puts half its worth into hits on the other team and half into health for the sender's, so two sides trading the same coins cancel out. A gift's worth grows 2% faster than its coins for every tenfold past 100, so bigger is a bit better per coin. Meteors come as a shower, one for about every 100 of hit, up to 12, then each hits harder and is drawn bigger; they smash through shields, and land on the next strongest ball if theirs is gone. Rage and a shield stack to two gifts' worth at a time; past that, a gift is its coins in HP. Before, a Rose healed 1 HP a coin while a Galaxy was one 30-damage meteor, so 60 Roses beat a Galaxy 59 times in 60. Run through the real show now: a Galaxy beats 60 Roses every time, a Galaxy against 1,000 Roses or 10 Hand Hearts comes out about even, whichever lands first and a couple of seconds apart, and a Universe beats a Galaxy every time (`tests/live.test.ts`). The number over a meteor is what the ball it landed on took, so one that falls on empty ground shows none, and a falling meteor is drawn over the ball it'll really land on.
- **No gift ends a round on the spot** (`hangOn` in `lib/battle/sim.ts`, `HANG_ON` in `lib/live/games/battle.ts`): a meteor that would knock out a team's last ball leaves it hanging on at 1 HP for 4 seconds of play (about 7 real ones, as it runs in slow motion), owing the rest of the hit. Anything that heals the team pays that off first, and once it's paid the team's saved; if it isn't, the team goes down. Nothing else hurts it while it hangs on, and the arena says how much it needs and for how long. Before, any meteor on a fresh round (Rivals' 299-coin Corgi, the battle's Galaxy) ended it within about a second, so whoever sent one first won outright, even against the same coins sent two seconds later. Videos don't hang on.
- **Rounds gifted big still end in a knockout**: once the walls close in, a wall takes 4% of a ball's health a touch (at least 1), so a ball gifted hundreds of HP wears down in time. Videos keep the old 1 a touch; it's an option on the world that only the LIVE games set.
- **Likes** (`likesToHp` in `lib/live/show.ts`): 30 likes are 1 HP while they come in at up to 5 a second (per team, or per viewer in the everyone-for-themselves games), measured over the last 10 seconds or so. Faster than that, each like is worth less in step, so likes add at most about 1 HP every 6 seconds however hard a crowd taps. At a flat 30 a HP, 150 likes a second per team pushed half the fights to time-outs and outweighed a Galaxy; now that many likes make a fight about ten seconds longer than one without, none go to time, and a Galaxy beats them.
- **Picking a side in chat**: 1 or 2, left or right, a team's name, or its first letter, so r is Red and b is Blue (r used to mean right, which is Blue). With a streamer's own names, their first letters; in Rivals, the rivalry's and red's and blue's. A letter that could mean either side (Bulls and Bears, or Ronaldo and red) means neither.
- **Abilities in the battle** come from a pool of five (spike, sword, grow, speed, heal). A streamer can switch any off but must keep two.
- **Ball Race** (`lib/live/games/race.ts`): typing "go" enters free with weight 1. After the gate opens, free entries wait for the next race. Gifters always get in, even when it's full (24 racers); a CPU makes room first, then the newest free entry. Three CPU racers fill an empty board. A race ends when the first ball finishes and the podium fills (3 finishers, or 2.5 seconds), or at 75 seconds. Every coin is weight already, so big gifts were never worth less per coin here; a gift worth two Galaxies or more adds a shockwave to the rocket, and three or more a ghost too, since a second rocket can't take a ball any further. The banner names those ("BLASTS DOWN AND KNOCKS EVERYONE BACK") rather than counting rockets that didn't fire.
- **Last One Standing** (`lib/live/games/royale.ts`): free entry for the first 15 seconds of a round, after that viewers wait for the next one. Gifters always get in and a CPU makes way. Four CPUs at 5 HP fill an empty arena; 24 fighters at most. Every coin is 1 HP already; a gift worth several Galaxies brings down a meteor on each of that many of the biggest rivals, up to five and never more than there are, so the banner's count is how many rivals it hit.
- **The default gift menu** is Rose (1 coin), Finger Heart (5), Perfume (20), Hand Hearts (100), Corgi (299) and Galaxy (1,000), shown at those prices. Each item starts a coin under for the big ones (99 and 999), so other gifts at those common prices count too.
- **Custom gift menus** (Creator) are a gift name and its price for each of the six items, not just prices, so the menu on screen always names a gift that really costs that much. The first gift must cost 1 coin and each one after more than the last; otherwise the defaults stand.
- **Settings and the game address**: the game reads a streamer's settings through a private key in its address (32 random characters). A new key can be made at any time; the old address then shows a bar asking for the new one and plays the free game. Changes reach a game that's already open when it's reloaded.
- **A database blip isn't an old address** (`app/live/play/page.tsx`): the settings are read three times before giving up, and only a key that really isn't anyone's shows the old-address bar. When they can't be read, the game plays with the last settings it loaded (kept in the browser source), or with none kept, plays the plain game, says RECONNECTING TO HOOKLINE, asks again every 30 seconds and starts over with the streamer's settings once they come.
- **Without a key** (someone trying it from the website) the game is the free one: the battle, with the tag. Demo mode (`?demo=1`) can show any game, since it's only pretend viewers.
- **Whose LIVE it was** (`lib/live/proof.ts`): the game can be pointed at anyone's LIVE, so coins only count as the streamer's when every LIVE the game heard is a TikTok account they connected. The bridge says which account it's on; the game reports every LIVE it heard real viewers on; the server checks those against the username TikTok itself gave when they connected (the `user.info.profile` scope, kept in `tiktok_usernames`, which only the server writes). TikFinity doesn't say whose LIVE it is, so its sessions can't be verified. Connecting TikTok later verifies the LIVEs already heard on it. A session that heard anyone else's LIVE, even for a moment, stays unverified.
- **Part of a LIVE through TikFinity**: the game says outright (`unknown`) when any of a session came from a source that didn't name the LIVE, such as TikFinity after the bridge was switched off mid-LIVE. The server keeps that on the session for good (`heard_unknown`), and such a session is never verified, even by connecting TikTok later. An empty list couldn't say this, since it also means "nothing new".
- **Usernames wait for TikTok** (`TIKTOK_PROFILE=1`): TikTok refuses the whole sign-in over a scope its app doesn't have, so Hookline only asks for `user.info.profile` once the founder has added it to the TikTok app. Until then no LIVE can be verified, and the LIVE page and setup say that plainly instead of asking people to connect TikTok again.
- **Gift coins seen, never earnings**: everywhere the figure shows (Earnings, Home, the LIVE page, recaps, admin) it's "gift coins seen": what viewers sent, as the game counted them, not what TikTok pays. Only verified LIVEs count; the rest are listed apart, labelled not verified, with why.
- **Sessions are the server's to write**: people can read their own `live_sessions` rows but not write them, so nobody can give themselves coins through Supabase's API. Only the key-checked route saves them.
- **Saying when it can't hear the LIVE** (`lib/live/connection.ts`): when TikFinity or the bridge isn't running (after 20 seconds' grace, counted from when the game started listening or the connection dropped, not from each retry) or the bridge is running but not connected to TikTok (no username, not live, a wrong username, an error), the orange bar says NOT CONNECTED TO YOUR LIVE. Five minutes with no chat, likes or gifts gets a small grey line instead, since that can be a quiet LIVE or TikFinity not signed in, and viewers shouldn't be alarmed by it. The details go beside the game, off the stream.
- **The bridge only talks to Hookline** (`lib/live/bridge.ts`): its WebSocket and status page answer only Hookline's own site (baked in at build time from `HOOKLINE_SITE`) and pages on the streamer's computer, addressed to the computer itself, so another website can't disconnect a game or point it at someone else's LIVE. Programs that aren't browsers are let in; they're already on the computer.
- **Switching every bridge off**: each bridge asks `/api/live/bridge` when it starts and every 10 minutes. With `BRIDGE_OFF=1` they let go of the LIVE and tell their games, which move to TikFinity by themselves; the setup pages offer TikFinity only. A bridge that can't reach Hookline keeps going, so an outage never stops a LIVE. TikFinity stays the default source either way.
- **Newer bridge**: the setup page compares the running bridge's version with the latest GitHub release and offers the download when it's behind.
- **The setup page looks only when asked**: looking for TikFinity and the bridge makes the browser ask about apps on the device, so the setup waits for a click (and remembers it) instead of asking everyone who reads the page.
- **Who the LIVE games are for** (`lib/live/eligibility.ts`): TikTok only lets people 18 and over go LIVE and get gifts, usually from about 1,000 followers, and the games need a computer (LIVE Studio or OBS). Before LIVE setup, three ticks: 18 or older, a TikTok that can go LIVE, a computer. Anyone missing one is told plainly they can't earn gifts with the games yet, pointed at Shorts, not shown the Pro upsell for more games, and Home leads them to Shorts. Hookline asks rather than checks. The copy says what TikTok needs and never promises that Shorts or the games will earn.
- **Session numbers count real viewers only.** Demo and test-panel viewers play like anyone but never count, and rounds count from the first thing a real viewer does, so testing before going live doesn't add up. The game sends the whole session so far every 30 seconds and as it closes; numbers only ever go up, and a session dated in the future or more than a week back is turned away.
- **A new session after 20 quiet minutes**: with nothing from a real viewer for 20 minutes, the next thing a real viewer does starts a new session, and rounds played in the quiet don't count. OBS keeps a browser source running between streams, so before this a game left open made several LIVEs one session and stopped saving a week after the first.

## Rivals

- **The game** (`lib/live/games/rivals.ts`, with its rules in `lib/live/rivals.ts`): the ball battle with named sides, red always on the left and blue on the right, and a different rivalry every round. The vote offers three that didn't just play; without a vote, the next one is random. Both sides field the same champion, a different one each round, so the gifts decide it. Everyone picks a side again each round, since last round's sides were different ones.
- **Each side's gifts**: the same three powers for both (a ball with the gifter's face, Rage 2X, a meteor), each set off by that side's own gift, so the gift picks the side, even for someone who typed the other number. Red: Paper Crane (99 coins), Sunglasses (199), Corgi (299). Blue: Little Crown (99), Hearts (199), Boxing Gloves (299). Each pair costs exactly the same, and all six are regular gifts that every price list puts at those prices with no gifter level needed. The Money Gun and Gem Gun (500) were left out because price lists disagree on which of them needs gifter level 15, and Hand Hearts and Confetti (100) because Confetti may be a level 10 reward. On Creator any of the six can be swapped; they must be six different gifts, each side's climbing in price, or Hookline's stand.
- **Any other gift** helps the side the giver is on (or the side that's behind, for someone who hasn't picked), by its price: under the cheapest power it heals 1 HP a coin, and from there it sets off the best power it reaches, like the battle's menu, worth its own coins. So one that costs more than the meteor brings down more of them: a Galaxy about three times a Corgi's, a Universe about 45 Galaxies'. It used to stop at the 299-coin meteor, so a Galaxy did what a Corgi did.
- **Gift names are matched loosely**: case, spaces and punctuation don't count, and a few gifts that turn up spelled two ways (Boxing Glove, Hand Heart, Donut) match either way.
- **Typing** 1 or 2, a side's name, or red or blue picks a side for free.
- **No score between rounds**, since the sides change: the top right says RIVALS where the battle shows its score.
- **Rivalries are choices, never who people are.** Hookline's come in groups: sports (clubs, NBA, NFL, F1), games and tech (consoles, phones), food and drink, cars, all ages, and politics (Republicans vs Democrats). Countries, religion, race, gender and who people love are off limits: none of the presets touch them, and a streamer's own rivalry that names one is refused when saved, and dropped if it's in saved settings anyway. TikTok's rules on hate protect exactly those things, and a LIVE that sets them against each other for gifts turns its chat on people and gets cut. There's no Places group any more, since where people are from is off limits; saved settings that still list it just drop it.
- **The check on a streamer's own** (`lib/live/offlimits.ts`) is a list built from data, not a handful of words: every country with its short names and the words for its people, continents and ethnic groups, religions with their figures, gods and books, sexual orientation and gender identity, and armed and hate groups. Words match whole and in the ways people type them (plurals, "Team Israel", "teamisrael", "U.S.A.", "Musl1ms", "Jewwws"), so "Real Madrid" and "Atlético" are fine. People named for coming from somewhere else (immigrants, illegals, foreigners, refugees) count, and so do slurs for race, faith and who people love, kept in the file in ROT13 so it doesn't read as a list of them. Every country's name is checked in Spanish, Portuguese, French, German, Italian, Dutch, Polish, Turkish, Indonesian and Romanian and in its own language ("Alemania", "Polska", "Sverige"), from the names the browser and server already know (`Intl.DisplayNames`); the words for peoples, faiths, race and who people love are checked in Spanish, Portuguese and French too, and in other languages not yet. Words those share with clubs, names and everyday things ("Granada", the Montreal "Canadiens", German "Island" for Iceland, "Cristiano") count only against another place or not at all, and "America" is Club América until it's against another place. Gender words and some figures' names count only on their own ("Women", "Team Jesus", not "Man United" or "Gabriel Jesus"); black, white and brown only next to "people" or each other ("Black vs White", not "Black Ops"); countries named like everyday things (Jordan, Turkey, Georgia) only against another place; and a few foods and breeds named after a people (French fries, Greek yogurt, German shepherd) read as the thing. Cities and states aren't checked, since clubs carry their names. Names in other alphabets, emoji, and letters that don't come down to A to Z (like ʘ) are refused until there's a check that can read them; small capitals from "fancy text" and letters like ı, ø and ß are read as the letters they look like, so "ɪɴᴅɪᴀ" is India. Ball Battle's own team names go through the same check (`sidesProblem` in `lib/live/rivals.ts`), and saved ones that fail go back to Red and Blue in the streamer's colours.
- **Grown-up by default**: only viewers 18 and over can send gifts, so the groups that start on are sports, games and tech, food and drink, and cars. Roblox, Minecraft, Fortnite, Mario and Sonic, cats and dogs, and summer and winter are in "All ages", off until a streamer turns it on.
- **Politics is off until a streamer turns it on**, with a warning beside it: TikTok is strict about LIVEs that stir up politics for gifts, and in 2024 it banned some creators who ran political fights from LIVE matches.
- **Plans**: Rivals comes with Pro, each side's own gifts with Creator (like the battle's own menu), and a streamer's own rivalries, up to 12 of them at 12 letters a side, with any plan that has Rivals.

## Accounts and billing

- **Signing in** is Supabase Auth: an email link or Google, no passwords. On a developer's own machine (`HOOKLINE_DEV_LOGIN=1` and not a production build) there's also a sign-in without email, so the app can be worked on offline. It can't be switched on in a deploy.
- **The database is reached directly** with postgres.js, not through Supabase's REST API. Each request runs in a transaction that takes on Supabase's `authenticated` role with the user's id, so row-level security decides what they can see and change. Webhooks, crons and the render worker run as the table owner.
- **Only Hookline's server reaches the tables** (`supabase/migrations/20261017050000_server_only.sql`): Supabase's REST and GraphQL APIs take the same sign-in and the same role, so without this anyone signed in could read and change their own rows directly, past every check the app makes (a Short's file and status, a LIVE game's rivalries). The app marks its own transactions (`hookline.via`, set in `asUser`), and every table in `public` has a restrictive policy that refuses anything signed in without that mark. A new table gets it in the migration that makes it, with `select public.server_only('public.<table>');`, and a database test fails on any table without it.
- **Secrets at rest** (OAuth tokens, a seller's Stripe key) are encrypted by the app with AES-256-GCM using `TOKEN_KEY`, and the tables holding them have no RLS policies, so nothing signed in can read them even by mistake.
- **Connected accounts are the server's to write** (`saveChannel` in `lib/server/channels-store.ts`): only connecting through Google or TikTok adds a channel or says which account it is. People can answer a channel's questions (made for kids, who sees new Shorts) and disconnect it, nothing else, since the "I posted it" check and the test-channel gate rely on which account it is.
- **Plans** (`lib/billing/plans.ts`): Pro makes 3 Shorts a day and Creator 5 a day per channel (the brief said "daily"). Yearly is 10 times monthly: two months free. A failed card (`past_due`) keeps the plan while Stripe retries; cancelled, unpaid or unfinished subscriptions drop to Free. A price that isn't one of ours never grants a plan.
- **Changing plans** goes through Stripe's billing portal once someone is paying, so nobody ends up with two subscriptions. Checkout is only for going from Free to paid.
- **Deleting an account** cancels the subscription at once (no refund for the rest of the period), then deletes the account and everything that cascades from it. Videos already posted stay on YouTube and TikTok.
- **Age**: 13 and over can use Hookline; under 18 needs a parent or guardian, and any paid plan is theirs. LIVE gifts follow TikTok's own age rules. Said in the terms and privacy pages.
- **Onboarding** is two taps: what you want (Shorts, LIVE games, or both), then connecting accounts, which can be skipped. "Shorts" lands on the Shorts queue, "LIVE" on the Live page, "both" on Home.
- **Times** in the app show in the viewer's own timezone, filled in on their device.

## Shorts

- **Six formats, all drawn by code**: ball battles, quizzes, would you rather, text stories, rankings and bar-chart races (`lib/shorts/formats/`). A Short is stored as its format and parameters (its look and ending included), so the video someone approves is exactly the one that's rendered and posted.
- **Text stories are off until a creator turns them on** (Settings, Shorts). YouTube's monetization policy names "templated storylines or scrolling text" as not payable, judged across the whole channel, and a text story is exactly that.
- **The content ledger** (`content_ledger`, `lib/server/shorts/ledger.ts`): every question (sums too), would-you-rather choice, story, ranking, race and fight (matchup and seed) is claimed by the account it's made for, and no other account ever gets it, because YouTube judges "the same situation over and over" across channels too. Claims are all or nothing on the table's key, so two accounts making Shorts at the same moment can't both get one; the loser makes something else. They're always taken in the same order, so two at once wait their turn instead of deadlocking. What was made before the ledger was entered into it, oldest first. A deleted account's claims stay, since its videos stay up. Swiped-away Shorts keep their claims too. A bank item keeps its id for good, even when it's reworded, since channels that had it are kept by the id.
- **Charts are claimed by how they come out**: a ranking by its figure, region and countries in order, a race by its figure, region and top countries at the start and the finish. Neighbouring years mostly rank the same countries the same way, and a race moved a few years mostly runs the same, so claiming the year or the stretch of years let different channels post the same video with other numbers. That leaves about 200 rankings and 220 races in the data, shared by every channel.
- **The same thing in other words**: questions, choices and stories are claimed with the words that carry their meaning, and anything Claude writes for a channel is checked against every channel's (the same test as within a channel: most of those words the same). Its id comes from those words, so the same words in another order are the same item and only one channel can have it.
- **Fresh material from Claude** (`lib/server/shorts/fresh.ts`): quiz questions, would-you-rathers (and text stories, where they're on) are written for each account by Claude as they're needed, from a few random subjects at a time, so the ledger doesn't starve anyone. Code checks every one (`lib/shorts/checks.ts`): length, no sums (those are only ever worked out by code), nothing unsafe or aimed at young kids, nothing asked before in other words. Quiz facts must be ones the model is certain of and that can't change, and a second call answers each question without being told the answer: only the ones it gets right, is sure of and says can't change are kept. Nothing the banks ask is written again, and no capitals at all, since the data asks every country's. Claude gets a minute for a channel's new material; after that the banks fill in, so the morning's Shorts never wait long on it. Without Claude set up, the built-in banks are used, and they run out as channels claim them.
- **For teens and adults**: no times tables (sums are 12 x 6 and up, percentages and squares), no spider legs or pet dragons. The built-in questions are general knowledge for grown-ups, and every country's capital the data has is asked as well (with other capitals on its continent as the wrong answers), except ones that are disputed, have moved lately or give themselves away.
- **Each channel's look** (`lib/shorts/look.ts`): one of 11 palettes (checked for contrast in the tests), three type choices (Anton, or Bricolage Grotesque in capitals or as written), five backdrops and square or round corners. Their counts share no factor, so look n and n+1 differ in all four, and past the first 330 the backdrop turns as well (a pattern is turned, a glow moves, a plain one gets a wash of the accent from that side), so the first 29,700 look numbers all draw differently; the tests compare what's drawn, not the numbers. Each account claims the lowest free number in the ledger the first time it makes a Short, and keeps it. Look 0 is Hookline's own, for the website and Shorts made before. It's per account until Shorts are made per channel.
- **Openings and endings** (`lib/shorts/timing.ts`): quizzes, would-you-rathers, rankings and races start on the action from the first frame, with the title over it for a second and a half, instead of a title card. Each Short ends one of three ways, picked at random: the comment screen, or a loop that holds the end a moment and slides the opening back in so a replay carries straight on, or (where `allowComments` is false, for Shorts made for kids) a close that doesn't ask for anything, and nothing in the video or its description asks for a comment.
- **Battle tunes**: each battle plays one of three public-domain tunes (Ode to Joy, Für Elise, In the Hall of the Mountain King), picked when it's made. Battles saved before keep Ode to Joy.
- **When there's nothing new to make**, the queue says so in plain words instead of "Nothing waiting", with which kinds ran out, and a notification says it once each time it happens. Battles never run out, so a day's Shorts are only short of what the plan allows when every format has. Trends' "make one like this" asks for one format, so when that one has run out it says so on Trends only: the queue and the bell are left alone, since everything else can still be made. It won't make a text story while they're off; it points to Settings instead.
- **Run many, keep the best**: a battle is the most dramatic of 240 simulated fights (six matchups the channel has had least, 40 runs each); a quiz or a would-you-rather the best of 160 sets of questions; a ranking the closest-fought of every figure, region and year whose countdown nobody has had (the latest year counts a little extra), with each figure and region once a decade on a channel; a race the stretch of years with the most overtakes out of about 640 tried, 20 to 60 years long, a short stretch played slower so it still runs about 20 seconds.
- **Nothing made up in the videos.** Would-you-rathers show no poll results; the comments are the answer. Text stories carry a STORY label on screen and in their facts for the title writer. Quiz facts are ones that don't change, every capital is checked against the country-codes data in the tests, and sums are worked out by the code.
- **Real data for charts**: World Bank population (1960 on) and GDP in current US dollars (1960 on), through the Frictionless Data mirrors on GitHub, because the World Bank's own API wasn't reachable when this was built. The source and the year are on screen. Rankings are made for any year at least 90% of countries have a figure for, and where every country at the top today has one, so nobody who'd be near the top is missing. GDP races start in 1990 or later, since too many countries have no earlier figures. Country names are the short ones people use (USA, UK, DR Congo, Ivory Coast).
- **Titles** are written by Claude from a list of facts about the video, with rules against invented numbers and claims, spoilers, and presenting stories as real. The model is set with `ANTHROPIC_MODEL`. When Claude isn't set up or a call fails, the format's own plain title is used, and the description is a plain line plus hashtags; the log says why each time. Effort is set to low. A request turned down while it's sent is asked again without it, and effort is only left off for that model if that works; nothing goes by the model's name, so a new one works the day it comes out (`lib/server/shorts/titles.ts`).
- **How many**: Free makes 3 Shorts in any 7 days; Pro 3 a day; Creator 5 a day for each connected YouTube channel (up to 5). A Short swiped away is replaced straight away and the replacement doesn't count, up to 10 replacements a day. Nothing a channel has had, posted or swiped away, comes round again, and nothing another channel has had ever comes at all (the content ledger). A battle's matchup comes round again once the channel has had every one, as a fight nobody has seen.
- **When to post**: a new channel starts at noon, 4pm and 7pm in its own timezone (set from the browser at sign-up, changeable in Settings). About one Short in five goes out at another hour between 7am and 10pm instead, three Shorts at each hour before trying the next (counting ones posted and still waiting for their 24-hour reading), so there's something to learn from. A tried hour takes the place of a usual time on that same day, and only where it fits two hours from everything else, so trying never pushes the rest later. Once hours have at least three results each, the channel's three best are used: each hour goes by its middle result, pulled toward usual like a format's score, picked at least two hours apart so all three fit in a day, and only once each has beaten another hour that was really tried. Each Short's "why" says which of the three it is: a starting guess, another hour being tried, or one of the best hours so far and how many Shorts that's from. A yes after the suggested time has passed gets the next free best (or starting) hour, and its "why" is rewritten to match. Posts are at least an hour out and two hours apart (`lib/shorts/times.ts`).
- **Free doesn't post for you**: an approved Short is rendered for download with its caption to copy. "I posted it" (with the YouTube link, optionally) marks it posted.
- **A pasted link only counts once it's checked** (`markPosted` in `lib/server/shorts/queue.ts`): YouTube's videos.list (1 quota unit) has to put the video on one of the creator's own connected channels, asked as that channel first (it sees its private videos) and then with the API key. A link to someone else's video, one YouTube doesn't show, or one YouTube rejected, couldn't process or deleted is refused with a plain reason, and one YouTube video can't be two Shorts. With no way to ask (no channel and no API key) the link is kept, marked unchecked, and left out of views and learning. Only the server can set a video's YouTube link and what YouTube said about it: a database trigger refuses those changes from anyone signed in.
- **YouTube before its API audit** (`YOUTUBE_AUDITED`, `YOUTUBE_TEST_CHANNELS`): YouTube keeps everything an unaudited app uploads private, so until the audit passes Hookline only uploads to the founder's test channels. Everyone else's approved Shorts render and wait as "Ready for you to post", with their best time, a download, an Open YouTube Studio link and "I posted it". `YOUTUBE_AUDITED=1` turns uploads on for every channel.
- **YouTube**: public Shorts upload as private with a publish time, and YouTube makes them public then, so posting doesn't depend on Hookline being up at that moment. One due within 15 minutes goes up public. Unlisted and private ones (the channel's choice, asked when it's connected) go up at their time, since YouTube only schedules public ones. Each upload keeps who it was for and its publish time, if it had one: it counts as posted from that time, or from the upload when it went up as it is, and its history only says "private until its time" when it went up that way. Quizzes, rankings and races use the Education category; the rest Entertainment.
- **Posted means YouTube said so** (`confirmYouTube` in `lib/server/shorts/post.ts`): once a Short's time comes, videos.list (1 unit for up to 50) is asked whether it's processed and who can see it, every 4 minutes until it answers. Only then is it posted and "Live on YouTube" sent. Rejected, failed and deleted ones are marked failed with YouTube's reason in plain words. One still private 30 minutes after its time, when the channel wanted it seen, is marked failed as "kept private" and never counted; trying it again checks again, in case the creator fixed it in YouTube Studio. One still processing a day later is given up on. Each is asked as the channel it went to, the only one that sees its private videos. When that channel isn't connected any more, the API key asks instead (it sees public and unlisted videos), and one it still can't find 30 minutes after its time is marked failed as "couldn't check", with how to fix it, never called deleted; trying it again checks it again rather than uploading it twice.
- **Every upload says it isn't synthetic media** (`status.containsSyntheticMedia: false`): the videos are drawn by code and nothing in them is realistic altered or synthetic footage of real people or events, which is what YouTube's label is for.
- **TikTok** has no way to schedule, so Shorts go to TikTok at their time. With direct posting on (`TIKTOK_DIRECT_POST=1`), a yes first asks TikTok's questions for that Short, as TikTok requires for every post: the account it's going to, who can see it (the creator picks; nothing is filled in), comments, Duet and Stitch (off until switched on, and greyed out when they're off in the creator's TikTok settings), and whether it promotes their own brand or someone else's, with TikTok's terms underneath. The answers stay with the Short and are checked again against what TikTok allows the account when it posts. "Not on TikTok" keeps it to YouTube. When TikTok turns a post down, the Short shows TikTok's reason and can be chosen again; outages are retried every half hour for a day, and a full daily cap for apps under review (`reached_active_user_cap`) moves it to the same time the next day. TikTok captions leave out YouTube's #shorts tag. Without direct posting, a ready Short can be sent to the creator's TikTok drafts to finish in the app.
- **Made for kids is the creator's to say** (`lib/shorts/youtube.ts`, `components/app/connect.tsx`): a connected YouTube channel is asked "Is this channel made for kids?" with nothing picked, and nothing posts to it until it's answered. The swipe card starts each Short at the channel's answer and lets the creator change it for that one. What's chosen is sent with every upload as `selfDeclaredMadeForKids`. Every answer, for a channel or a Short, is written to `audience_log` by the database itself (who, which, when), and only the server can read it. YouTube's own verdict is read back too, for the channel when it's connected and for each Short when it's checked, and shown as "YouTube marked this Made for Kids: comments are off".
- **Made for kids means no comments**: a Short approved as made for kids, or going to a channel YouTube itself has set as made for kids, gets `params.allowComments = false`, so its ending doesn't ask for comments that YouTube will have turned off. Shorts kept before the channel answered take its answer when it comes (`fitAudience` in `lib/server/shorts/queue.ts`), and any that will be made for kids are made again without that ending, including one being made at that moment.
- **TikTok gets a copy without the free plan's tag**: TikTok's sharing rules are reported to forbid app watermarks on what's shared through its API, so every TikTok route (drafts and direct posts) sends a copy without "MADE WITH HOOKLINE". A tagged Short is rendered twice when its owner has TikTok connected; one made before that gets its tag-free copy from a render job of its own when it's sent. Downloads and YouTube keep the tag on Free.
- **The learning loop reads YouTube views**, only of public Shorts known to be on the creator's own channel (uploaded by Hookline, or a link checked with YouTube). TikTok's views show in Earnings, but TikTok posts aren't matched to their own view counts yet.
- **The learning loop**: views are read at 1 hour, 24 hours and 3 days (up to two days late if the worker was down). At 24 hours a Short is compared with the channel's typical Short, the median of its last 30 at 24 hours, once there are at least 3: (views + 10) / (typical + 10). A reading taken late is scaled back to 24 hours by the square root of how late it was, for the Short and for the baseline, and a late one never sends a "Nx your usual views" note. That moves a weight for its format and its kind (a battle's two powers, a quiz's subject, a story's tone, a chart's figure), averaged in log space over the last 20 results. Formats are drawn by these weights, each pulled toward usual by how few results it has (it counts for half at 8 results and never more than 20/28, `steady()` in `lib/shorts/learn.ts`), so one lucky Short moves a format's share by at most about a third; never below a fifth of the best, and no more than two of one format a day. Within a format, the best kind of three runs is kept. The queue quotes what a format or kind really averaged, and only calls it doing well or badly by the steadier figure.
- **The worker** (`pnpm worker`) does everything long or periodic: rendering, each channel's morning Shorts (from 6am local), posting every minute and views every 15 minutes. The morning run takes 25 accounts every 5 minutes (5 a run through `/api/cron/shorts`), longest waiting first, and writes down each try, how it went and when to try next in `shorts_runs`: tomorrow once it's made them or there's nothing new left; when Free's week frees up (the oldest of the week's turning a week old), or tomorrow if that's sooner; and after an hour when it went wrong. Timezones the database doesn't know count as UTC. The same jobs can be run from a scheduler through `/api/cron/post`, `/api/cron/stats` and `/api/cron/shorts`; each video is locked while it's being posted, so both at once is safe. The scheduler's Shorts job makes up to five accounts' Shorts a call and starts nobody new after two and a half minutes, inside the route's five-minute limit; an account whose Shorts fail is written down to be tried again in an hour, and the rest carry on, in the worker too.
- **YouTube's upload quota** belongs to the whole app. When an upload is refused for quota, that Short and the rest of that run's line wait until the quota resets (midnight Pacific time, plus five minutes), and the queue and the Short's page say "Waiting for YouTube upload quota" and when it goes up, instead of trying every minute. A Short whose time passes while it waits goes public as soon as it's up. Once the reset has passed, or the next try fails for another reason, it stops saying it's waiting.
- **Rendered files** go in a private Supabase Storage bucket called `videos` (made automatically the first time) and are handed out as 10-minute signed links. Without Supabase they're kept in `.data/videos` and served by the app to their owner.

## Trends

- **YouTube only**, through the official Data API. TikTok doesn't offer trending data to apps like this (its Research API is for academic researchers), so there's no TikTok trends.
- **Ten niches** (`lib/trends/niches.ts`), six of them one per Shorts format so "make one like this" can make one; the other four (satisfying, facts, gaming, football) are there to watch. People pick which to see; none picked means all.
- **Rising means growing fast**: views per hour since upload. Each niche is searched every four hours for Shorts (three minutes or less) uploaded in the last two days, and the 25 growing fastest are kept for a week; Rising now shows the last three days. A search costs 100 of the 10,000 daily quota units, so ten niches every four hours uses about 6,000.
- **Best times** come from the channel's own Shorts: each one's views at 24 hours against the channel's usual, by day of the week and three-hour block in its own timezone. YouTube doesn't share when a channel's viewers are online through its API.
- **Formats right now** is the middle growth rate of the rising Shorts in each format over three days, next to how that format does on the channel.
- **"Make one like this"** makes a Short of the same format straight into the queue, and counts toward the plan's Shorts.

## Earnings

- **Real figures only, from where they are**: YouTube Analytics (daily views, new subscribers and, for channels that can read it, YouTube's estimated revenue, refreshed for the last 35 days every 6 hours); TikTok's view counts (it only gives each video's total, so a day's views are the rise since the last reading, and a day without a reading shows as unknown, not zero); the gift coins seen on the streamer's own verified LIVEs, labelled as what viewers sent as the game counted it, never as money, with unverified LIVEs shown apart and not counted; and Stripe sales for people who sell something.
- **Stripe** needs a restricted key (`rk_live_…`) that can read balance transactions; a secret key is refused. It's stored encrypted. Sales are charges and payments less refunds, before Stripe's fees, kept per currency.
- **YouTube runs 2 to 3 days behind**: days after the last one YouTube has reported, and before its first, are "not reported yet" or unknown, never 0 views or $0; a day skipped between reported days is 0. Each YouTube card says how far it's reported up to.
- **Stripe is read back 35 days** from when it's connected, so days before that are unknown, not $0, and the card says where its figures start.
- **Charts**: one measure per chart, side by side rather than two on one axis, as daily bars from zero, with each day's exact figure on hover and in a table for screen readers.
- **Goals** are set on any of those measures, for this month (in the account's timezone) or all time.

## Making a battle the same fight everywhere

- Browsers don't agree on `Math.sin` and `Math.cos` to the last digit (Chrome changed its implementation after the version in Node 22), and a physics simulation turns that into a different fight: about a third of test fights played out differently in Chrome than in Node. So the battle simulation uses its own `sin`, `cos` and `hypot` (`lib/battle/trig.ts`), ports of fdlibm, which is what Node 22 uses. They match Node bit for bit (checked on 3.3 million inputs), so every fight is the same one it always was, and now the browser preview, the renderer and the server all compute the identical fight. `tests/trig.test.ts` pins the exact bits, and `tests/battle.test.ts` checks that every matchup still makes exactly the same fights.

## The platform around the sections

- **Notifications only for things that happened**: a Short going live on YouTube or out to TikTok, one that couldn't be made or was turned down, nothing new left to make, one ready to download (when nothing will post it), the morning's Shorts, a Short whose first day beat the usual by half again or more (worse results only steer what's made, they don't notify), a LIVE's recap once it's been quiet for 20 minutes, plan changes, and accounts needing reconnecting. Each event has a key, so it notifies once however many jobs notice it. Opening the bell counts them as seen. There are no emails yet; that needs an email service and is in NEXT.md's "Later".
- **Ctrl K (⌘K on a Mac)** opens a palette to go anywhere or do the common things (connect an account, switch the theme, sign out), matching any word typed against each command's name and the words people use for it.
- **Toasts** say what just happened after an action that doesn't change the page much: a swipe ("Posting Tue 4:00 PM" only when something will post it, otherwise "Kept. Best time to post it: Tue 4:00 PM"), a saved title, TikTok choices.
- **Nothing says a Short posts unless something will post it**: on Free, and before YouTube's audit except on test channels, the swipe card, the queue, the calendar, each Short's history and its "why" call its time the best time to post it, and Home's setup list and the note on going Pro don't promise posting.
- **The calendar** shows each Short on the local day and hour it posts or posted, in the account's own timezone, Sunday first. On a phone it's a list of days instead of a grid.
- **The library** shows every Short said yes to, newest first, 24 at a time, with views at the latest checkpoint and the first day against the usual Short, using the learning loop's own measure. The usual is the middle of the last 30 first days read on time, so it's views a first day really had; a first day read late is scaled back the way the learning loop does it and says "about". A Short's page labels each reading with how long after posting it was really taken.
- **What's working** draws each format's first-day result against the usual as one bar from a middle line, on a scale where twice and half are the same distance away, up to four times either way. A kind of Short (a power, a subject, a tone) is only called out after 3 results and when it's at least 15% away from usual.
- **Each Short's page** lists only what happened or is booked to, from the times saved as it happens (made, approved, rendered, uploaded, public, TikTok); nothing is inferred.
- **The worker checks in** every 30 seconds; one silent for 2 minutes counts as stopped. People only hear about it when their own Shorts are waiting to be made.
- **The admin page** (`/admin`) is for the emails in `ADMIN_EMAILS`; anyone else gets the ordinary not-found page. Revenue there is at list price: active plans and ones whose card Stripe is retrying, yearly plans as a twelfth, trials not counted. Stripe has the figures after fees and discounts.
- **On a phone's home screen**, Hookline opens straight into the app, in its own window.
