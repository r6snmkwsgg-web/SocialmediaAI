# Decisions

Defaults picked while building, so nobody has to guess why something is the way it is. Change any of them; each says where it lives.

## Order of work

- The account system (Phase 2) was built before streamer settings and session stats (Phase 1, items 3 and 4), because saving settings "per account" needs accounts. Everything else in Phase 1 came first.

## Live

- **The vote** (`lib/live/show.ts`): it runs on the win screen, which is 10 seconds long when voting is on and 7 when it's off. One vote per viewer, and typing again changes it. A gift during the vote adds 1 vote per coin with no cap, so a Galaxy is 1,000 votes, the same as 1,000 Roses (it was capped at 50, which made a Galaxy worth 50 Roses); those votes stay with the gifter and count once they type A, B or C. Ties are broken at random. When nobody votes, the next round is picked at random and nothing is announced.
- **Every coin counts about the same** (`lib/live/games/battle.ts`, `worth` in `lib/live/menu.ts`): in the battle and Rivals a coin is about 1 HP, whatever the gift. A Rose heals 1 HP; a ball carries its gift's coins as health; rage and the shield heal 1 HP a coin on top of the power; a meteor gift puts half its worth into hits on the other team and half into health for the sender's, so two sides trading the same coins cancel out. A gift's worth grows 2% faster than its coins for every tenfold past 100, so bigger is a bit better per coin. Meteors come as a shower, one for about every 100 of hit, up to 12, then each hits harder and is drawn bigger; they smash through shields, and land on the next strongest ball if theirs is gone. Rage and a shield stack to two gifts' worth at a time; past that, a gift is its coins in HP. Before, a Rose healed 1 HP a coin while a Galaxy was one 30-damage meteor, so 60 Roses beat a Galaxy 59 times in 60. Run through the real show now: a Galaxy beats 60 Roses every time, a Galaxy against 1,000 Roses or 10 Hand Hearts comes out about even, whichever lands first and a couple of seconds apart, and a Universe beats a Galaxy every time (`tests/live.test.ts`). The number over a meteor is what the ball it landed on took, so one that falls on empty ground shows none, and a falling meteor is drawn over the ball it'll really land on.
- **No gift ends a round on the spot** (`hangOn` in `lib/battle/sim.ts`, `HANG_ON` in `lib/live/games/battle.ts`): a meteor that would knock out a team's last ball leaves it hanging on at 1 HP for 4 seconds of play (about 7 real ones, as it runs in slow motion), owing the rest of the hit. Anything that heals the team pays that off first, and once it's paid the team's saved; if it isn't, the team goes down. Nothing else hurts it while it hangs on, and the arena says how much it needs and for how long. Before, any meteor on a fresh round (Rivals' 299-coin Corgi, the battle's Galaxy) ended it within about a second, so whoever sent one first won outright, even against the same coins sent two seconds later. Videos don't hang on.
- **Rounds gifted big still end in a knockout**: once the walls close in, a wall takes 4% of a ball's health a touch (at least 1), so a ball gifted hundreds of HP wears down in time. Videos keep the old 1 a touch; it's an option on the world that only the LIVE games set.
- **Likes** (`likesToHp` in `lib/live/show.ts`): 30 likes are 1 HP while they come in at up to 5 a second (per team, or per viewer in the everyone-for-themselves games), measured over the last 10 seconds or so. Faster than that, each like is worth less in step, so likes add at most about 1 HP every 6 seconds however hard a crowd taps. At a flat 30 a HP, 150 likes a second per team pushed half the fights to time-outs and outweighed a Galaxy; now that many likes make a fight about ten seconds longer than one without, none go to time, and a Galaxy beats them.
- **Picking a side in chat**: 1 or 2, left or right, a team's name, or its first letter, so r is Red and b is Blue (r used to mean right, which is Blue). With a streamer's own names, their first letters; in Rivals, the rivalry's and red's and blue's. A letter that could mean either side (Bulls and Bears, or Ronaldo and red) means neither.
- **Abilities in the battle** come from a pool of five (spike, sword, grow, speed, heal). A streamer can switch any off but must keep two.
- **Ball Race** (`lib/live/games/race.ts`): typing "go" enters free with weight 1. After the gate opens, free entries wait for the next race. Gifters always get in, even when it's full (24 racers); a CPU makes room first, then the newest free entry. Three CPU racers fill an empty board. A race ends when the first ball finishes and the podium fills (3 finishers, or 2.5 seconds), or at 75 seconds. Every coin is weight already, so big gifts were never worth less per coin here; a gift worth two Galaxies or more adds a shockwave to the rocket, and three or more a ghost too, since a second rocket can't take a ball any further. The banner names those ("BLASTS DOWN AND KNOCKS EVERYONE BACK") rather than counting rockets that didn't fire.
- **Last One Standing** (`lib/live/games/royale.ts`): free entry for the first 15 seconds of a round, after that viewers wait for the next one. Gifters always get in and a CPU makes way. Four CPUs at 5 HP fill an empty arena; 24 fighters at most. Every coin is 1 HP already; a gift worth several Galaxies brings down a meteor on each of that many of the biggest rivals, up to five and never more than there are, so the banner's count is how many rivals it hit.
- **The default gift menu** is Rose (1 coin), Finger Heart (5), Perfume (20), Hand Hearts (100), Corgi (299) and Galaxy (1,000), shown at those prices. Each item starts a coin under for the big ones (99 and 999), so other gifts at those common prices count too.
- **Custom gift menus** (Creator) are a gift name and its price for each of the six items, not just prices, so the menu on screen always names a gift that really costs that much. The first gift must cost 1 coin and each one after more than the last; otherwise the defaults stand.
- **Settings and the game address**: the game reads a streamer's settings through a private key in its address (32 random characters). A new key can be made at any time; the old address then shows a bar asking for the new one and plays the free game. Changes reach a game that's already open when it's reloaded. Hiding viewers' names is the one change that reaches an open game without a reload, within a minute.
- **A database blip isn't an old address** (`app/live/play/page.tsx`): the settings are read three times before giving up, and only a key that really isn't anyone's shows the old-address bar. When they can't be read, the game plays with the last settings it loaded (kept in the browser source), or with none kept, plays the plain game, says RECONNECTING TO HOOKLINE, asks again every 30 seconds and starts over with the streamer's settings once they come.
- **Without a key** (someone trying it from the website) the game is the free one: the battle, with the tag. Demo mode (`?demo=1`) can show any game, since it's only pretend viewers.
- **Whose LIVE it was** (`lib/live/proof.ts`): the game can be pointed at anyone's LIVE, so coins only count as the streamer's when every LIVE the game heard is a TikTok account they connected. The bridge says which account it's on; the game reports every LIVE it heard real viewers on; the server checks those against the username TikTok itself gave when they connected (the `user.info.profile` scope, kept in `tiktok_usernames`, which only the server writes). TikFinity doesn't say whose LIVE it is, so its sessions can't be verified. Connecting TikTok later verifies the LIVEs already heard on it. A session that heard anyone else's LIVE, even for a moment, stays unverified.
- **Part of a LIVE through TikFinity**: the game says outright (`unknown`) when any of a session came from a source that didn't name the LIVE, such as TikFinity after the bridge was switched off mid-LIVE. The server keeps that on the session for good (`heard_unknown`), and such a session is never verified, even by connecting TikTok later. An empty list couldn't say this, since it also means "nothing new".
- **Usernames wait for TikTok** (`TIKTOK_PROFILE=1`): TikTok refuses the whole sign-in over a scope its app doesn't have, so Hookline only asks for `user.info.profile` once the founder has added it to the TikTok app. Until then no LIVE can be verified, and the LIVE page and setup say that plainly instead of asking people to connect TikTok again.
- **Gift coins seen, never earnings**: everywhere the figure shows (Earnings, Home, the LIVE page, recaps, admin) it's "gift coins seen": what viewers sent, as the game counted them, not what TikTok pays. Only verified LIVEs count; the rest are listed apart, labelled not verified, with why.
- **Sessions are the server's to write**: people can read their own `live_sessions` rows but not write them, so nobody can give themselves coins through Supabase's API. Only the key-checked route saves them.
- **Saying when it can't hear the LIVE** (`lib/live/connection.ts`): when TikFinity or the bridge isn't running (after 20 seconds' grace, counted from when the game started listening or the connection dropped, not from each retry) or the bridge is running but not connected to TikTok (no username, not live, a wrong username, an error), the orange bar says NOT CONNECTED TO YOUR LIVE. Five minutes with no chat, likes or gifts gets a small grey line instead, since that can be a quiet LIVE or TikFinity not signed in, and viewers shouldn't be alarmed by it. The details go beside the game, off the stream.
- **The bridge only talks to Hookline** (`lib/live/bridge.ts`): its WebSocket and status page answer only Hookline's own site (baked in at build time from `HOOKLINE_SITE`) and pages on the streamer's computer, addressed to the computer itself, so another website can't disconnect a game or point it at someone else's LIVE. Programs that aren't browsers are let in; they're already on the computer.
- **Switching every bridge off**: each bridge asks `/api/live/bridge` when it starts and every 10 minutes. With `BRIDGE_OFF=1` they let go of the LIVE and tell their games, which move to TikFinity by themselves; the setup pages offer TikFinity only. A bridge that can't reach Hookline keeps going, so an outage never stops a LIVE. TikFinity stays the default source either way.
- **Newer bridge**: the setup page compares the running bridge's version with the latest GitHub release and offers the download when it's behind.
- **The setup page looks only when asked**: looking for TikFinity and the bridge makes the browser ask about apps on the device, so the setup waits for a click (and remembers it) instead of asking everyone who reads the page.
- **Who the LIVE games are for** (`lib/live/eligibility.ts`): TikTok only lets people 18 and over go LIVE and get gifts, usually from about 1,000 followers, and the games need a computer (LIVE Studio or OBS). Before LIVE setup, three ticks: 18 or older, a TikTok that can go LIVE, a computer. Anyone missing one is told plainly they can't earn gifts with the games yet, pointed at Shorts, not shown the Pro upsell for more games, and Home leads them to Shorts. Hookline asks rather than checks. The copy says what TikTok needs and never promises that Shorts or the games will earn.
- **Session numbers count real viewers only.** Demo and test-panel viewers play like anyone but never count, and rounds count from the first thing a real viewer does, so testing before going live doesn't add up. The game sends the whole session so far every 30 seconds and as it closes; numbers only ever go up, and a session dated in the future or more than a week back is turned away.
- **A new session after 20 quiet minutes**: with nothing from a real viewer for 20 minutes, the next thing a real viewer does starts a new session, and rounds played in the quiet don't count. OBS keeps a browser source running between streams, so before this a game left open made several LIVEs one session and stopped saving a week after the first.
- **Nothing heard after a LIVE counts against it** (`heardIn` in `lib/live/proof.ts`): once a session has been quiet for 20 minutes, what the game hears is noted only once the next session begins, with the next like, gift or pick. A join, a hello or a second follow starts no session. Noted under the last one, any of them heard from someone else's LIVE or through TikFinity would unverify it for good, taking its coins out of Earnings and its recap card and welcome backs with them.
- **Top gifters on screen** (`top3()` in `lib/live/show.ts`, `components/live/draw/top3.ts`): every game keeps the LIVE's top 3 gifters in a strip under the cards, round after round, by gift coins, with ties to whoever gave first. Only real viewers count. Once a LIVE has been quiet for 20 minutes the strip says TOP GIFTERS THIS LIVE until someone gifts on the next one, so a game left open never shows the last LIVE's gifters as this one's. Test-panel and demo gifts show only when they're sent while no LIVE is going, and go the moment a real viewer does anything. To make room, the cards gave up 8 px, the strip under them 4 px and each menu row 6 px (8 px in Rivals); the arena and the fights didn't move. On the win screen the strip stays where it is, or moves under the winner when there's a vote. It never asks anyone for a gift.
- **Welcome backs** (`greet` in `lib/live/show.ts`, `lastLive` in `lib/server/live.ts`): when one of the last LIVE's top 3 gifters does anything at the next one, joining included, the game says WELCOME BACK, KAI: TOP SUPPORTER LAST LIVE (or IN THE TOP 3 LAST LIVE), once that LIVE. The last LIVE is the newest one that's been quiet for 20 minutes and had a round or a gift, and only one verified as the streamer's own counts, since anyone else's LIVE has someone else's supporters. Nobody is welcomed back to the LIVE they're giving in. Reloading the game starts a new session in the middle of a LIVE, so a session begun within 20 minutes of another's last event carries that LIVE on. And someone who joins the list while a LIVE is going waits for the next one, since the server can't hear the joins and hellos that keep a LIVE going. Until `TIKTOK_PROFILE=1` no LIVE is verified, so nobody is welcomed back. The game is sent their TikTok ids, never their names, and asks again every 30 seconds; the name on screen comes from the LIVE itself. A LIVE begins with the first thing a real viewer does after 20 quiet minutes. Welcomes go up during play only, one at a time, and not until 8 seconds after the newest big gift arrived; a big gift that lands while one is up sends it back to wait, so a gift's moment never hides one. Nothing on screen ties a welcome to gifting or promises the top gifters anything, since TikTok restricts incentives for gifts.
- **Hiding viewer names** (`nameOf` in `components/live/draw/kit.ts`, the On screen card on `/app/live`): one tap swaps every viewer's name in the game for initials (K.S., or "a viewer" for a name with no letters) and every profile picture for a letter; pictures aren't even fetched. It's applied as each frame is drawn, so everything on screen changes at once, and it's on every plan. An open game asks every 30 seconds, so it changes within a minute, and it keeps the choice with the settings it saves in the browser source, so a reload while Hookline is down still hides names. Saving the other settings never undoes it: the settings form sends back what it loaded with, so the stored choice wins.
- **Recap cards** (`components/live/draw/recap.ts`, `/app/live/<id>`): each LIVE has its own page, linked from the LIVE page and from its recap notification. Once a LIVE verified as the streamer's own is over, the page draws a 1080 × 1920 picture to post: their username, the day and game, the gift coins seen (what viewers sent), rounds, the most playing at once and the top 3 supporters, with a switch to show them as initials that starts from the game's setting. It's drawn in the browser, so nothing is rendered or kept on the server, and on Free it carries MADE WITH HOOKLINE. Everything but the tag sits clear of where TikTok's app covers a photo (estimates in `RECAP_CLEAR`). A LIVE that isn't verified gets no card, since it could show someone else's numbers as the streamer's, and its page says why. There's no money or currency anywhere on it.
- **Supporters' names** (`lib/live/supporters.ts`): kept only as each session's top 5 gifters (TikTok id, display name and coins), shown to the streamer alone, and deleted with the account. The recap notification names nobody, so there's no copy anywhere else, and only ids reach the game. Deleting a single LIVE, an off switch for the top 3 and welcome backs, and masking offensive names automatically are follow-ups; until then, hiding names is the fix for a name that shouldn't stay on screen.

## Rivals

- **The game** (`lib/live/games/rivals.ts`, with its rules in `lib/live/rivals.ts`): the ball battle with named sides, red always on the left and blue on the right, and a different rivalry every round. The vote offers three that didn't just play; without a vote, the next one is random. Both sides field the same champion, a different one each round, so the gifts decide it. Everyone picks a side again each round, since last round's sides were different ones.
- **Each side's gifts**: the same three powers for both (a ball with the gifter's face, Rage 2X, a meteor), each set off by that side's own gift, so the gift picks the side, even for someone who typed the other number. Red: Paper Crane (99 coins), Sunglasses (199), Corgi (299). Blue: Little Crown (99), Hearts (199), Boxing Gloves (299). Each pair costs exactly the same, and all six are regular gifts that every price list puts at those prices with no gifter level needed. The Money Gun and Gem Gun (500) were left out because price lists disagree on which of them needs gifter level 15, and Hand Hearts and Confetti (100) because Confetti may be a level 10 reward. On Creator any of the six can be swapped; they must be six different gifts, each side's climbing in price, or Hookline's stand.
- **Any other gift** helps the side the giver is on (or the side that's behind, for someone who hasn't picked), by its price: under the cheapest power it heals 1 HP a coin, and from there it sets off the best power it reaches, like the battle's menu, worth its own coins. So one that costs more than the meteor brings down more of them: a Galaxy about three times a Corgi's, a Universe about 45 Galaxies'. It used to stop at the 299-coin meteor, so a Galaxy did what a Corgi did.
- **Gift names are matched loosely**: case, spaces and punctuation don't count, and a few gifts that turn up spelled two ways (Boxing Glove, Hand Heart, Donut) match either way.
- **Typing** 1 or 2, a side's name, or red or blue picks a side for free.
- **No score between rounds**, since the sides change: the top right says RIVALS where the battle shows its score.
- **Rivalries are choices, never who people are.** Hookline's come in groups: sports (clubs, NBA, NFL, F1), games and tech (consoles, phones), food and drink, cars, all ages, and politics (Republicans vs Democrats). Countries, religion, race, gender and who people love are off limits: none of the presets touch them, and a streamer's own rivalry that names one is refused when saved, and dropped if it's in saved settings anyway. TikTok's rules on hate protect exactly those things, and a LIVE that sets them against each other for gifts turns its chat on people and gets cut. There's no Places group any more, since where people are from is off limits; saved settings that still list it just drop it.
- **The check on a streamer's own** (`lib/live/offlimits.ts`) is a list built from data, not a handful of words: every country with its short names and the words for its people, continents and ethnic groups, religions with their figures, gods and books, sexual orientation and gender identity, and armed and hate groups. Words match whole and in the ways people type them (plurals, "Team Israel", "teamisrael", "U.S.A.", "Musl1ms", "Jewwws"), so "Real Madrid" and "Atlético" are fine. People named for coming from somewhere else (immigrants, illegals, foreigners, refugees) count, and so do slurs for race, faith and who people love, kept in the file in ROT13 so it doesn't read as a list of them. Every country's name is checked in Spanish, Portuguese, French, German, Italian, Dutch, Polish, Turkish, Indonesian and Romanian and in its own language ("Alemania", "Polska", "Sverige"), from the names the browser and server already know (`Intl.DisplayNames`); the words for peoples, faiths, race and who people love are checked in Spanish, Portuguese and French too, and in other languages not yet. Words those share with clubs, names and everyday things ("Granada", the Montreal "Canadiens", German "Island" for Iceland, "Cristiano") count only against another place or not at all, and "America" is Club América until it's against another place. Gender words and some figures' names count only on their own ("Women", "Team Jesus", not "Man United" or "Gabriel Jesus"); black, white and brown only next to "people" or each other ("Black vs White", not "Black Ops"); countries named like everyday things (Jordan, Turkey, Georgia) only against another place; and a few foods and breeds named after a people (French fries, Greek yogurt, German shepherd) read as the thing. Cities and states aren't checked, since clubs carry their names. Names in other alphabets, emoji, and letters that don't come down to A to Z (like ʘ) are refused until there's a check that can read them; small capitals from "fancy text" and letters like ı, ø and ß are read as the letters they look like, so "ɪɴᴅɪᴀ" is India. Ball Battle's own team names go through the same check (`sidesProblem` in `lib/live/rivals.ts`), and saved ones that fail go back to Red and Blue in the streamer's colours.
- **Grown-up by default**: only viewers 18 and over can send gifts, so the groups that start on are sports, games and tech, food and drink, and cars. Roblox, Minecraft, Fortnite, Mario and Sonic, cats and dogs, and summer and winter are in "All ages", off until a streamer turns it on.
- **Politics is off until a streamer turns it on**, with a warning beside it: TikTok is strict about LIVEs that stir up politics for gifts, and in 2024 it banned some creators who ran political fights from LIVE matches.
- **Plans**: Rivals comes with Pro, each side's own gifts with Creator (like the battle's own menu), and a streamer's own rivalries, up to 12 of them at 12 letters a side, with any plan that has Rivals.

## LIVE clips

- **A round played back exactly** (`lib/live/tape.ts`, `replay` in `lib/live/clip.ts`): a Ball Battle or Rivals round is kept as a small record of what reached its arena: the arena's seed and rules, the champions and their health, and every heal, ball, rage, shield and meteor with the physics step it landed on. The arena uses only arithmetic, `Math.sqrt` and its own trig, so the server, the worker and the player each play the same fight back from the record alone. The show around the arena runs on real time and draws its own random numbers, so it can't be played back, which is why the record is taken at the arena and not from the chat. Rounds recorded in Node 22 played back bit for bit in headless Chromium 141, and the other way round; `pnpm replay-check` checks it again against any Chromium (`CHROME_PATH`), such as one of the version OBS's browser source runs.
- **A fingerprint on every round**: the record carries a hash of the arena on the step the round was decided (every ball's place, speed, angle and health, both sides' timers and what they owe, and the meteors falling). It's checked when the round comes in, again when a morning takes it and again in the worker before the video is made, so a change to how rounds play is caught instead of drawing a different fight. A deliberate change to the arena or the live battle bumps `CLIP_V`, and rounds from before are turned away. The pinned round in `tests/fixtures/clip-rivals.json` fails first.
- **Only Ball Battle and Rivals**: Ball Race and Last One Standing use the engine's own maths and the show's shared random numbers, so they can't be played back exactly yet, and rounds where every ball is a viewer would need drawing with names hidden. The LIVE page says so when one of them is picked.
- **Real viewers' rounds only** (`keepClip` in `lib/live/show.ts`): a round is kept only when the session counted it, something a real viewer did reached the arena and no demo or test-panel viewer touched it, with at least 15 seconds of fight, and never a Politics rivalry. Its number is the session's own count of rounds, so a game left open in OBS for days still says "Round 14 of your LIVE", never how many it has played in all. A game opened without a streamer's key never keeps one.
- **What's worth a Short** (`clipDrama` and `clipWindow` in `lib/live/clip.ts`): lead changes, a comeback, a save, big gifts and a close finish score points, and a round decided on time, or a draw, loses some. A round needs 4: a comeback with a big gift, two lead changes, or a save with a close finish. A clip always ends on the result: a fight of 15 to 40 seconds is shown whole, a longer one as its best last 20 to 40 seconds. The bar is a guess, so turned-away rounds keep why, and the dull ones their score, for three days to tune it by.
- **The record is the game's word, like gift coins** (`saveClip` in `lib/server/clips.ts`): the game runs in the streamer's own browser, so it can't hold a secret to sign with. Hookline checks a round hangs together (the same arena, fed those calls, is decided on that step with that result) and keeps the rules (the live game's own arena and health, and sides and rivalries that pass the same checks as saved settings). It takes rounds only through the streamer's key, played back one at a time and at most 120 an hour, so a burst of them can't hold up the server, and a morning only takes one from a finished LIVE of the same game that counts as theirs, played while that LIVE was going. It can't prove every gift in it happened.
- **Each LIVE's best three, for three days** (`saveClip`, `tidyClips`): only each LIVE's 3 best rounds wait for a morning, and rounds go three days after they came, waiting or not. A round over 128,000 bytes or 6,000 inputs isn't sent. Turning "Best rounds become Shorts" off deletes the rounds waiting.
- **One a day, within the plan** (`takeClip` in `lib/server/clips.ts`, `make` in `lib/server/shorts/generate.ts`): a waiting round takes one of the day's Shorts, at most one a day, on every plan, Free's 3 a week included. YouTube's uploads are shared by every account, the posting times are planned for the plan's number, and "3 Shorts a day" stays true. It goes to the account's first channel that gets Shorts, in its look, and never as a replacement or from "make one like this". The whole day goes in at once or not at all, so a failure is tried again in an hour, and a try at a round that goes wrong, or a round that stops waiting while its day is being made, costs nothing: the day is all made Shorts.
- **Who's named** (`withoutNames` in `lib/live/clip.ts`, `showable` in `lib/live/clip-rules.ts`): a round names only the people who sent gifts in it, by display name, never a viewer id or a picture. With names hidden on the stream, it names nobody. Names Hookline can't read, or that name people for who they are, are blanked. Its video names nobody unless the creator ticks "Show gifters' names" on its card; otherwise the names are let go of when it's kept without them, swiped away or left unswiped for three days, so they stay only in a Short that shows them.
- **No coins, gifts or prices** (`remotion/shorts/Clip.tsx`): a big gift's banner says what its power did ("5 METEORS ON BLUE"), never the gift's name, price or coins. The title writer is told not to mention gifts or coins, ask anyone to send anything or name any viewer, and a written title or description that talks about gifts, coins or sending is swapped for the plain one (`writeCopy` in `lib/server/shorts/titles.ts`), so a clip can never read as asking for a gift. The health in a record is the arena's own number, shown as health, as it was on the LIVE.
- **Redrawn, and credited**: a clip is the same fight drawn by code in the channel's own look, with the LIVE's own sides and colours, outlined so they read on any look, and "From my LIVE" at the top and the end. It isn't a recording of the LIVE. It uploads as Entertainment, like battles, and goes through the same swipe, made-for-kids answer and TikTok form as every Short.
- **Only from the streamer's own LIVE** (`clipsAllowed` in `lib/server/clips.ts`): no round becomes a Short until `TIKTOK_PROFILE=1`, since until then no LIVE counts as anyone's, and none from a LIVE heard through TikFinity. Accounts set to LIVE games only get none, since the morning run doesn't make their Shorts. The LIVE page says why when rounds can't become Shorts.

## YouTube Live

- **A third way to hear a LIVE** (`lib/server/ytlive.ts`, `app/api/live/youtube/route.ts`): with `?src=youtube` the game asks Hookline, and while something asks, Hookline reads the chat of the account's own stream through its connected channel, at most every 10 seconds (or YouTube's own interval when that's longer), and hands what's new to every open game. Each reply says when to ask next, so a game asks about once a read. Nothing to install, nothing held open, no worker: each request makes at most one YouTube call, with an 8-second limit, never inside a transaction. Each account has one reader, and a turn is claimed on its row by the database's clock, so OBS and a preview tab together never ask YouTube twice.
- **Reading costs quota** (`ACCOUNT_LIVE_UNITS` in `lib/shorts/quota.ts`, `takeLiveUnits` in `lib/server/quota.ts`): a read is 5 units, so a busy hour is about 1,800 and a quiet one about 1,000 (after 3 empty reads it slows to every 20 seconds, except while a game counts down or votes). Every call is counted as YouTube Live's, inside its 25% of the day's units; each account may use 1,500 a quota day (about 50 minutes of busy chat), and Free accounts pause at 80% of YouTube Live's share, so paying accounts keep the rest. When either runs out, or YouTube says the quota is used, the game says until when, in the streamer's own time. That leaves room for one or two streamers a day until Google grants more (NEXT.md, section 11).
- **Finding the stream**: only on the account's own channel, with its own access (liveBroadcasts.list for every kind of stream, counted at 5 units until its cost is checked): every minute for 10 minutes, every 5 minutes up to 3 hours, then every 30, and it never stops, since OBS keeps a game open between streams. OBS starting the stream, a reload or Check now looks straight away, all of them together at most once a minute. A stream that went offline but that YouTube still lists (its auto-stop is off) is read again on the same schedule, counted from when it went offline, so a game left open after a stream costs no more than one left open before it; only a read says it's back. A stream's earlier chat is history, never news, and a game that fell behind gets only the last minute, never old votes and Super Chats in another round. The streamer picks which of their channels the game reads; one that needs reconnecting, or a token that wasn't let see the account, asks for a reconnect instead of failing.
- **Power by colour, never money** (`tierPower` in `lib/live/youtube.ts`): a Super Chat's power comes from YouTube's own tier, its colour (100, 200, 500, 1,000, 2,000, 5,000 and 10,000, then 10,000 more for each red tier above), the same in every currency, since YouTube sets each currency's colours itself. A Jewel is 1 and a new or gifted membership 500. Power plays the part of coins in every rule, and never leaves the game: the stream shows colours and POWER, never an amount, a currency or a tier number. Jewels combos pay only the rise, however YouTube sends the steps.
- **YouTube's menu** (`youtubeMenu`, `YT_SIDE_MENU`): each game keeps its six effects, set off by Jewels gifts for the three small ones (drawn as thresholds, 1+, 5+ and 20+, since no one gift is claimed to cost that) and by Blue, Light blue and Yellow Super Chats for the big ones; Rivals' sides both take Jewels, Blue, Light blue and Green. Nothing sent on YouTube says which side it's for, so the chat picks it, a Super Chat's own comment first, so "2" in it picks blue. A streamer's own TikTok gift menu doesn't apply.
- **Behind the stream** (`LAG` in `lib/live/show.ts`): on YouTube the vote, Ball Race's countdown and Last One Standing's free entry run 15 seconds longer (one read plus the stream's delay), and while a game counts down or votes the reader doesn't slow down for a quiet chat.
- **Words on the stream** (`lib/live/words.ts`): every line that differs by platform is in one place. Nothing on a YouTube stream mentions likes, shares, coins or TikTok gifts, since YouTube's chat has none of them, and the banner names the gift as it was sent, on TikTok too.
- **Whose LIVE** (`recordSession` in `lib/server/live.ts`): a YouTube session counts as the streamer's when every stream it names is one their own reader heard on their own channel, which is the only way the game hears YouTube. It keeps no gift coins (the database refuses any) and its top supporters by name only, in the game's order. What the game heard is the stream's count, never the session's, since one stream can run across two sessions, and a send can't move a session to the other platform. Welcome backs, recap cards and LIVE clips stay TikTok's for now; a YouTube LIVE's recap says what the game heard on its stream, and for someone whose Super Chats Hookline reads, points to Earnings for what viewers paid, without saying when, since that read can wait for YouTube's quota.
- **Who can play on YouTube** (`ytLiveFit` in `lib/live/eligibility.ts`): four ticks, asked rather than checked, like TikTok's: 18 or older, a channel that can go live, a computer, and Super Chat, which comes with the Partner Program. YouTube lets people go live alone from 16, but the games run on Super Chats, and a stream without them would show a menu nobody can send. Anyone missing one is told why and pointed at Shorts, and isn't sold the games on YouTube's account. Under 18, the age is the only reason given for Super Chats, since nobody younger can switch Super Chat on.
- **The LIVE page listens only when asked** (`components/live/youtube-setup.tsx`): it shows what the reader last knew, and Check now asks Hookline to look for the stream, then lists what it hears for 10 minutes and stops, since reading costs quota. Its asks keep the chat read but never count as a game being open.
- **What's kept**: chat for up to 10 minutes, so open games can pick it up; each stream with what the game heard on it until its channel is disconnected; YouTube viewers' names and channels in saved sessions for 30 days (`sweepYouTubeLive`, run with the LIVE recaps), or until the channel the game heard them through is disconnected, since they were read with its access. Whatever a read or a look brings back after the channel was disconnected meanwhile is dropped.

## Accounts and billing

- **Signing in** is Supabase Auth: an email link or Google, no passwords. On a developer's own machine (`HOOKLINE_DEV_LOGIN=1` and not a production build) there's also a sign-in without email, so the app can be worked on offline. It can't be switched on in a deploy.
- **The database is reached directly** with postgres.js, not through Supabase's REST API. Each request runs in a transaction that takes on Supabase's `authenticated` role with the user's id, so row-level security decides what they can see and change. Webhooks, crons and the render worker run as the table owner.
- **Only Hookline's server reaches the tables** (`supabase/migrations/20261017050000_server_only.sql`): Supabase's REST and GraphQL APIs take the same sign-in and the same role, so without this anyone signed in could read and change their own rows directly, past every check the app makes (a Short's file and status, a LIVE game's rivalries). The app marks its own transactions (`hookline.via`, set in `asUser`), and every table in `public` has a restrictive policy that refuses anything signed in without that mark. A new table gets it in the migration that makes it, with `select public.server_only('public.<table>');`, and a database test fails on any table without it.
- **Secrets at rest** (OAuth tokens, a seller's Stripe key) are encrypted by the app with AES-256-GCM using `TOKEN_KEY`, and the tables holding them have no RLS policies, so nothing signed in can read them even by mistake.
- **Connected accounts are the server's to write** (`saveChannel` in `lib/server/channels-store.ts`, `disconnectChannel` in `lib/server/channels.ts`): only connecting through Google or TikTok adds a channel or says which account it is, and only the server disconnects one. People can answer a channel's questions (made for kids, who sees new Shorts) and say whether it gets Shorts, nothing else, since the "I posted it" check and the test-channel gate rely on which account it is. Disconnecting is soft: the channel stays, marked disconnected, with its Shorts, look, learning and schedule, so connecting it again carries on where it stopped. Its keys, its TikTok username and the figures read with its access are deleted at once, since the creator took that access back. When it's the TikTok Shorts go to, TikTok choices for Shorts not sent yet are cleared too, since TikTok's form showed that account; an extra one from before there was one a plan leaves them alone, and Settings only says they were cleared when they were. Anything else read with a channel's access is deleted by its own package's trigger on `channels` when the channel is disconnected. Google or TikTok shutting Hookline out marks a connected channel as needing reconnecting, never one the creator disconnected.
- **Plans** (`lib/billing/plans.ts`): Free makes 3 Shorts a week and Pro 3 a day, for one YouTube channel; Creator makes 5 a day for each of up to 5 (the brief said "daily"). Every plan has one TikTok account: before TikTok's audit at most 5 accounts across the whole app can post in a day, every post needs TikTok's own form, which would then need a which-account choice, and TikTok takes about 15 posts a day from an account. TikToks connected before that rule stay connected, posts go to the first, and new extras are refused. Yearly is 10 times monthly: two months free. A failed card (`past_due`) keeps the plan while Stripe retries; cancelled, unpaid or unfinished subscriptions drop to Free. A price that isn't one of ours never grants a plan.
- **Changing plans** goes through Stripe's billing portal once someone is paying, so nobody ends up with two subscriptions. Checkout is only for going from Free to paid.
- **Deleting an account** cancels the subscription at once (no refund for the rest of the period), then deletes the account and everything that cascades from it. Videos already posted stay on YouTube and TikTok.
- **Age**: 13 and over can use Hookline; under 18 needs a parent or guardian, and any paid plan is theirs. LIVE gifts follow TikTok's own age rules. Said in the terms and privacy pages.
- **Onboarding** is two taps: what you want (Shorts, LIVE games, or both), then connecting accounts, which can be skipped. "Shorts" lands on the Shorts queue, "LIVE" on the Live page, "both" on Home.
- **Times** in the app show in the viewer's own timezone, filled in on their device.

## Shorts

- **Six formats, all drawn by code**: ball battles, quizzes, would you rather, text stories, rankings and bar-chart races (`lib/shorts/formats/`). A Short is stored as its format and parameters (its look and ending included), so the video someone approves is exactly the one that's rendered and posted.
- **Text stories are off until a creator turns them on** (Settings, Shorts). YouTube's monetization policy names "templated storylines or scrolling text" as not payable, judged across the whole channel, and a text story is exactly that.
- **The content ledger** (`content_ledger`, `lib/server/shorts/ledger.ts`): every question (sums too), would-you-rather choice, story, ranking, race and fight (matchup and seed) is claimed by the account it's made for, and no other account ever gets it, because YouTube judges "the same situation over and over" across channels too. Claims are all or nothing on the table's key, so two accounts making Shorts at the same moment can't both get one; the loser makes something else. They're always taken in the same order, so two at once wait their turn instead of deadlocking. What was made before the ledger was entered into it, oldest first. A deleted account's claims stay, since its videos stay up. Swiped-away Shorts keep their claims too. A bank item keeps its id for good, even when it's reworded, since channels that had it are kept by the id.
- **Charts are claimed by how they come out**: a ranking by its figure, region and countries in order, a race by its figure, region and top countries at the start and the finish. Neighbouring years mostly rank the same countries the same way, and a race moved a few years mostly runs the same, so claiming the year or the stretch of years let different channels post the same video with other numbers. That leaves about 200 rankings and 220 races in the data, shared by every channel.
- **The same thing in other words**: questions, choices and stories are claimed with the words that carry their meaning, and anything Claude writes for a channel is checked against every channel's (the same test as within a channel: most of those words the same). Its id comes from those words, so the same words in another order are the same item and only one channel can have it.
- **Fresh material from Claude** (`lib/server/shorts/fresh.ts`): quiz questions, would-you-rathers (and text stories, where they're on) are written for each account by Claude as they're needed, from a few random subjects at a time, so the ledger doesn't starve anyone. Code checks every one (`lib/shorts/checks.ts`): length, no sums (those are only ever worked out by code), nothing unsafe or aimed at young kids, nothing asked before in other words. Quiz facts must be ones the model is certain of and that can't change, and a second call answers each question without being told the answer: only the ones it gets right, is sure of and says can't change are kept. Nothing the banks ask is written again, and no capitals at all, since the data asks every country's. Claude gets a minute for a channel's new material; after that the banks fill in, so the morning's Shorts never wait long on it. Without Claude set up, the built-in banks are used, and they run out as channels claim them.
- **For teens and adults**: no times tables (sums are 12 x 6 and up, percentages and squares), no spider legs or pet dragons. The built-in questions are general knowledge for grown-ups, and every country's capital the data has is asked as well (with other capitals on its continent as the wrong answers), except ones that are disputed, have moved lately or give themselves away.
- **Each channel's look** (`lib/shorts/look.ts`): one of 11 palettes (checked for contrast in the tests), three type choices (Anton, or Bricolage Grotesque in capitals or as written), five backdrops and square or round corners. Their counts share no factor, so look n and n+1 differ in all four, and past the first 330 the backdrop turns as well (a pattern is turned, a glow moves, a plain one gets a wash of the accent from that side), so the first 29,700 look numbers all draw differently; the tests compare what's drawn, not the numbers. Each channel claims the lowest free number in the ledger the first time it makes a Short, and keeps it, even while it's disconnected; an account with no channel has one of its own, which its first channel takes over (`lookFor` in `lib/server/shorts/ledger.ts`). Look 0 is Hookline's own, for the website and Shorts made before.
- **Openings and endings** (`lib/shorts/timing.ts`): quizzes, would-you-rathers, rankings and races start on the action from the first frame, with the title over it for a second and a half, instead of a title card. Each Short ends one of three ways, picked at random: the comment screen, or a loop that holds the end a moment and slides the opening back in so a replay carries straight on, or (where `allowComments` is false, for Shorts made for kids) a close that doesn't ask for anything, and nothing in the video or its description asks for a comment.
- **Battle tunes**: each battle plays one of three public-domain tunes (Ode to Joy, Für Elise, In the Hall of the Mountain King), picked when it's made. Battles saved before keep Ode to Joy.
- **When there's nothing new to make**, the queue says so in plain words instead of "Nothing waiting", with which kinds ran out, and a notification says it once each time it happens. Battles never run out, so a day's Shorts are only short of what the plan allows when every format has. Trends' "make one like this" asks for one format, so when that one has run out it says so on Trends only: the queue and the bell are left alone, since everything else can still be made. It won't make a text story while they're off; it points to Settings instead.
- **Run many, keep the best**: a battle is the most dramatic of 240 simulated fights (six matchups the channel has had least, 40 runs each), and the title writer is told just that, never that nobody picks the winner, since keeping the most dramatic run decides who wins in most matchups (a Ball League match is one fight, never picked); a quiz or a would-you-rather the best of 160 sets of questions; a ranking the closest-fought of every figure, region and year whose countdown nobody has had (the latest year counts a little extra), with each figure and region once a decade on a channel; a race the stretch of years with the most overtakes out of about 640 tried, 20 to 60 years long, a short stretch played slower so it still runs about 20 seconds.
- **Nothing made up in the videos.** Would-you-rathers show no poll results; the comments are the answer. Text stories carry a STORY label on screen and in their facts for the title writer. Quiz facts are ones that don't change, every capital is checked against the country-codes data in the tests, and sums are worked out by the code.
- **Real data for charts**: World Bank population (1960 on) and GDP in current US dollars (1960 on), through the Frictionless Data mirrors on GitHub, because the World Bank's own API wasn't reachable when this was built. The source and the year are on screen. Rankings are made for any year at least 90% of countries have a figure for, and where every country at the top today has one, so nobody who'd be near the top is missing. GDP races start in 1990 or later, since too many countries have no earlier figures. Country names are the short ones people use (USA, UK, DR Congo, Ivory Coast).
- **Titles** are written by Claude from a list of facts about the video, with rules against invented numbers and claims, spoilers, and presenting stories as real. The model is set with `ANTHROPIC_MODEL`. When Claude isn't set up or a call fails, the format's own plain title is used, and the description is a plain line plus hashtags; the log says why each time. Effort is set to low. A request turned down while it's sent is asked again without it, and effort is only left off for that model if that works; nothing goes by the model's name, so a new one works the day it comes out (`lib/server/shorts/titles.ts`).
- **How many** (`allowancesOf` in `lib/server/shorts/generate.ts`): Free makes 3 Shorts in any 7 days; Pro 3 a day; Creator 5 a day for each YouTube channel that gets Shorts (up to 5). Each channel counts its own, and the account never gets more than the plan's total for all of them together, counting Shorts made today for a channel since disconnected or switched off, so swapping or reconnecting a channel adds nothing that day. A Short swiped away is replaced straight away on its own channel and the replacement doesn't count, up to 10 replacements a day for each channel (so up to 50 on a full Creator account); one for a channel that doesn't get Shorts any more isn't replaced. Nothing a channel has had, posted or swiped away, comes round again, and nothing another channel has had ever comes at all (the content ledger), not even another channel of the same account. A battle's matchup comes round again once the channel has had every one, as a fight nobody has seen.
- **A Short is for one channel** (`videos.channel_id`, `supabase/migrations/20261018010000_channels.sql`): each Short is made for one of the account's YouTube channels, in its look, at its times, with its made-for-kids answer, and only ever uploads to that one. Only the server moves one: when a pasted link shows it's on another of the creator's channels (its views count there, and a link on one they disconnected is refused), and when an account's first channel takes over the Shorts it had before it had one, except those already on YouTube, since which channel they're on isn't known. A Short can only name a channel of its own owner (a foreign key on the channel and its owner together), and uploads join the channel on the Short's owner too: those two are what keep a Short off anyone else's channel, and a trigger keeps people from moving their own. An account with no channel works as it always did. With more than one channel, the queue, the swipe card, the calendar, the library and each Short's page say which channel each Short is for, and the queue, the calendar and the library show one channel's Shorts on their own when it's picked (`?channel=`). A Short's page says when its channel is disconnected or needs reconnecting, and what connecting it again would do: post it there only where Hookline would.
- **Channels beyond the plan** (`gettingShorts` in `lib/shorts/channels.ts`): the plan's room goes to the YouTube channels connected first that are switched on (one connected again after being disconnected goes to the end of the line; one that only needs reconnecting keeps its place, and keeps getting Shorts). The rest, and any the creator switched off in Settings, get no new Shorts but stay connected for link checks, views and Earnings, and Shorts kept for them still post. Settings and the queue say which channels get none and why. A full plan can't connect another, though one of theirs that needs reconnecting always can. Channels connected before there were Shorts for each channel keep the order posting used then: the connected ones first, then any that needed reconnecting, so the channel an account's Shorts went to keeps getting them.
- **When to post**: each channel has its own times, learned from its own Shorts and two hours apart from its own other Shorts, whatever another channel has. A new channel starts at noon, 4pm and 7pm in the account's timezone (set from the browser at sign-up, changeable in Settings), and with Creator's 5 a day at 10am, noon, 4pm, 7pm and 9pm. About one Short in five goes out at another hour between 7am and 10pm instead, three Shorts at each hour before trying the next (counting ones posted and still waiting for their 24-hour reading), so there's something to learn from. A tried hour takes the place of a usual time on that same day, and only where it fits two hours from everything else, so trying never pushes the rest later. Once hours have at least three results each, the channel's three best are used (five on Creator, once six hours have three results each): each hour goes by its middle result, pulled toward usual like a format's score, picked at least two hours apart so all of them fit in a day, and only once each has beaten another hour that was really tried. Each Short's "why" says which it is: a starting guess, another hour being tried, or one of the best hours so far and how many Shorts that's from. A yes after the suggested time has passed gets the next free best (or starting) hour, and its "why" is rewritten to match. So do a channel's kept Shorts whose time passed while it was away, once it's connected again (or connected for the first time, taking over the account's), and those whose time passed while it waited for its first made-for-kids answer, once it has one, instead of all going up at once (`retimeLate` in `lib/server/shorts/queue.ts`); Settings says how many moved after a connect. That's only where Hookline uploads them itself: anywhere else nothing goes up by itself, so the times the creator saw stay. Connecting saves the channel and gives those Shorts their new times together, so nothing can go up to it in between, and each account books its times one at a time (`lockTimes`), so two at once keep their gaps. Posts are at least an hour out and two hours apart (`lib/shorts/times.ts`).
- **Free doesn't post for you**: an approved Short is rendered for download with its caption to copy. "I posted it" (with the YouTube link, optionally) marks it posted.
- **A pasted link only counts once it's checked** (`markPosted` in `lib/server/shorts/queue.ts`): YouTube's videos.list (1 quota unit) has to put the video on one of the creator's own connected channels, asked as that channel first (it sees its private videos) and then with the API key. A link to someone else's video, one YouTube doesn't show, or one YouTube rejected, couldn't process or deleted is refused with a plain reason, and one YouTube video can't be two Shorts. With no way to ask (no channel and no API key) the link is kept, marked unchecked, and left out of views and learning. Only the server can set a video's YouTube link and what YouTube said about it: a database trigger refuses those changes from anyone signed in.
- **YouTube before its API audit** (`YOUTUBE_AUDITED`, `YOUTUBE_TEST_CHANNELS`): YouTube keeps everything an unaudited app uploads private, so until the audit passes Hookline only uploads to the founder's test channels. Everyone else's approved Shorts render and wait as "Ready for you to post", with their best time, a download, an Open YouTube Studio link and "I posted it". `YOUTUBE_AUDITED=1` turns uploads on for every channel.
- **YouTube**: public Shorts upload as private with a publish time, and YouTube makes them public then, so posting doesn't depend on Hookline being up at that moment. They go up at most a day before their time, so each day's uploads go to the Shorts due soonest (`postDue` in `lib/server/shorts/post.ts`). One due within 15 minutes goes up public. Unlisted and private ones (the channel's choice, asked when it's connected) go up at their time, since YouTube only schedules public ones. Each upload keeps who it was for and its publish time, if it had one: it counts as posted from that time, or from the upload when it went up as it is, and its history only says "private until its time" when it went up that way. Quizzes, rankings and races use the Education category; the rest Entertainment.
- **Posted means YouTube said so** (`confirmYouTube` in `lib/server/shorts/post.ts`): once a Short's time comes, videos.list (1 unit for up to 50) is asked whether it's processed and who can see it, every 4 minutes for its first half hour (from its time, or from the upload if that was later) and then every half hour, until it answers (`checkAgain` in `lib/shorts/youtube.ts`). A check that fails waits the same way, so one YouTube won't answer for isn't asked every minute. Only then is it posted and "Live on YouTube" sent. Rejected, failed and deleted ones are marked failed with YouTube's reason in plain words. One still private 30 minutes after its time, when the channel wanted it seen, is marked failed as "kept private" and never counted; trying it again checks again, in case the creator fixed it in YouTube Studio. One still processing a day later is given up on. Each is asked as the channel it went to, the only one that sees its private videos. When that channel isn't connected any more, the API key asks instead (it sees public and unlisted videos), and one it still can't find 30 minutes after its time is marked failed as "couldn't check", with how to fix it, never called deleted; trying it again checks it again rather than uploading it twice.
- **Every upload says it isn't synthetic media** (`status.containsSyntheticMedia: false`): the videos are drawn by code and nothing in them is realistic altered or synthetic footage of real people or events, which is what YouTube's label is for.
- **TikTok** has no way to schedule, so Shorts go to TikTok at their time. With direct posting on (`TIKTOK_DIRECT_POST=1`), a yes first asks TikTok's questions for that Short, as TikTok requires for every post: the account it's going to, who can see it (the creator picks; nothing is filled in), comments, Duet and Stitch (off until switched on, and greyed out when they're off in the creator's TikTok settings), and whether it promotes their own brand or someone else's, with TikTok's terms underneath. The answers stay with the Short and are checked again against what TikTok allows the account when it posts. "Not on TikTok" keeps it to YouTube. When TikTok turns a post down, the Short shows TikTok's reason and can be chosen again; outages are retried every half hour for a day, and a full daily cap, for apps under review (`reached_active_user_cap`) or on the account itself (`spam_risk_too_many_posts`), moves it to the same time the next day. Every channel's Shorts go to the account's one TikTok, so its posts keep a 2-hour gap of their own across all of them, booked one at a time, between 7am and 10pm, which is at most 8 a day, under TikTok's cap of about 15 (`tiktokTime` in `lib/shorts/tiktok.ts`): a Short whose time is within two hours of another TikTok post goes there a little later, and the note after a yes gives TikTok's time when TikTok is what posts it. A Short made for a YouTube channel stays ready for that channel once TikTok has it; only one with no YouTube channel counts as posted when TikTok takes it. TikTok captions leave out YouTube's #shorts tag. Without direct posting, a ready Short can be sent to the creator's TikTok drafts to finish in the app, at most 5 a day by TikTok's rule, and TikTok's reason shows in plain words when it turns one down.
- **Made for kids is the creator's to say** (`lib/shorts/youtube.ts`, `components/app/connect.tsx`): a connected YouTube channel is asked "Is this channel made for kids?" with nothing picked, and nothing posts to it until it's answered. The swipe card starts each Short at the channel's answer and lets the creator change it for that one. What's chosen is sent with every upload as `selfDeclaredMadeForKids`. Every answer, for a channel or a Short, is written to `audience_log` by the database itself (who, which, when), and only the server can read it. YouTube's own verdict is read back too, for the channel when it's connected and for each Short when it's checked, and shown as "YouTube marked this Made for Kids: comments are off".
- **Made for kids means no comments**: a Short approved as made for kids, or going to a channel YouTube itself has set as made for kids, gets `params.allowComments = false`, so its ending doesn't ask for comments that YouTube will have turned off. Shorts kept before the channel answered take its answer when it comes (`fitAudience` in `lib/server/shorts/queue.ts`), and any that will be made for kids are made again without that ending, including one being made at that moment.
- **TikTok gets a copy without the free plan's tag**: TikTok's sharing rules are reported to forbid app watermarks on what's shared through its API, so every TikTok route (drafts and direct posts) sends a copy without "MADE WITH HOOKLINE". A tagged Short is rendered twice when its owner has TikTok connected; one made before that gets its tag-free copy from a render job of its own when it's sent. Downloads and YouTube keep the tag on Free.
- **The learning loop reads YouTube's plays and engaged views**, only of public Shorts known to be on the creator's own channel (uploaded by Hookline, or a link checked with YouTube): plays from the Data API at 1 hour, 24 hours and 3 days, and engaged views from YouTube Analytics once YouTube has reported a Short's first two days (`lib/server/shorts/proof.ts`). TikTok's views show in Earnings, but TikTok posts aren't matched to their own view counts yet.
- **The learning loop**: each channel learns from its own Shorts. Plays are read at 1 hour, 24 hours and 3 days (up to two days late if the worker was down). A Short's first day is compared with its own channel's usual first day, the median of its last 30 at 24 hours, once there are at least 3: (plays + 10) / (usual + 10), so a small channel is measured against its own audience, not a big one's. Once YouTube Analytics has reported the Short's engaged views, it goes by those instead (YouTube Analytics and the Partner Program, below), and each first day is learned once, by whichever it went by. A reading taken late is scaled back to 24 hours by the square root of how late it was, for the Short and for the baseline, and a late one never sends a "Nx your usual first day" note. That moves a weight for its format and its kind (a battle's two powers, a quiz's subject, a story's tone, a chart's figure), averaged in log space over the last 20 results, for the channel and for the account as a whole (which an account with no channel, Trends' formats, Home and the library across all channels go by). Trends' best times measure each Short against its own channel's usual too, and the library's What's working for one channel shows that channel's own results against its own usual (`insightsFor` in `lib/server/shorts/library.ts`), calling out a kind only by its own results. Formats are drawn by these weights, each pulled toward usual by how few results it has (it counts for half at 8 results and never more than 20/28, `steady()` in `lib/shorts/learn.ts`), so one lucky Short moves a format's share by at most about a third; never below a fifth of the best, and no more than two of one format a day. A channel new to an account with others is pulled toward what those found instead of usual, their results pooled and counting as 8 of its own, so it starts from what worked there and its own results take over as they come (`withPrior` in `lib/shorts/learn.ts`, `weightsFor` in `lib/server/shorts/weights.ts`); its "why" says what the other channels found, never with a figure, since none would be its own. Within a format, the best kind of three runs is kept. The queue quotes what a format or kind really averaged, and only calls it doing well or badly by the steadier figure.
- **The worker** (`pnpm worker`) does everything long or periodic: rendering, each channel's morning Shorts (from 6am local), posting every minute and views every 15 minutes. The morning run takes 25 channels every 5 minutes (5 a run through `/api/cron/shorts`), each channel that gets Shorts on its own, or an account with no channel as one, longest waiting first. A Creator account's channels are made one after another, in turn under the account's lock, and the morning's note goes out once a day for the account, after its first channel's, so with several it doesn't say how many came. It writes down each channel's try, how it went and when to try next in `shorts_runs`: tomorrow once it's made them or there's nothing new left; when Free's week frees up (the oldest of the week's turning a week old), or tomorrow if that's sooner; and after an hour when it went wrong. Timezones the database doesn't know count as UTC. The same jobs can be run from a scheduler through `/api/cron/post`, `/api/cron/stats` and `/api/cron/shorts`; each video is locked while it's being posted, so both at once is safe. The scheduler's Shorts job makes up to five channels' Shorts a call and starts nobody new after two and a half minutes, inside the route's five-minute limit; a channel whose Shorts fail is written down to be tried again in an hour, and the rest carry on, in the worker too.
- **YouTube's upload quota** belongs to the whole app. When an upload is refused for quota, or Hookline's own count says the day's uploads are used up (the same limit, so the same words), that Short and the rest of that run's line wait until the quota resets (midnight Pacific time, plus five minutes), and the queue and the Short's page say "Waiting for YouTube upload quota" and when it goes up, instead of trying every minute. One whose account has had its plan's uploads for the day says that instead, with the plan and how many it uploads a day, until the reset; after a move to Free it reads like any Free Short (`HELD` in `lib/shorts/history.ts`). A Short whose time passes while it waits goes public as soon as it's up. Once the reset has passed, or the next try fails for another reason, it stops saying it's waiting. An upload that fails for any other reason that passes is tried again in half an hour, not every minute, since every try counts against everyone's uploads: it shows the error rather than a wait, and TikTok still gets it at its time.
- **Rendered files** go in a private Supabase Storage bucket called `videos` (made automatically the first time) and are handed out as 10-minute signed links. Without Supabase they're kept in `.data/videos` and served by the app to their owner.

## Niches and the Ball League

- **One niche per channel** (`lib/shorts/niches.ts`, `channels.niche`): each YouTube channel can be about one thing the creator picks beside its other settings: Ball League, Football clubs, Formula 1, NBA, Cars, Gaming, Money and economies, or Geography and history. An account with no channel picks for its own Shorts (`profiles.niche`), and its first channel takes that over, with its Ball League unless the channel has one of its own; after that the account's own Shorts are about nothing of their own, so a league left with them stops its season, and its matches never go onto a channel with another cast (`adoptOrphans` in `lib/server/channels.ts`). A channel then only gets its niche's formats, quiz kinds, chart figure and written material, and learns alongside the account's other channels about the same thing; channels that haven't picked learn together (`peersOf` in `lib/server/shorts/weights.ts`). Nothing is picked for anyone: until a channel picks, it gets a mix of everything, and the queue says so. Football clubs is marked Suggested when Claude is set up, otherwise Geography and history. Ball League is never suggested: ball battles are the closest match to YouTube's example of the same situation over and over, and named cartoon balls lean made for kids.
- **What each niche runs on**: Football clubs, Formula 1, NBA, Cars and Gaming are written by Claude alone, so they can only be picked once Claude is set up; when Claude doesn't answer, that channel makes nothing that time and tries again in an hour, and its note says why. If Claude stops being set up, nothing new can be written, so it tries the next morning instead. Money and economies and Geography and history also use Hookline's own charts and questions, which every channel shares and which run out (population has 67 rankings and 163 races, GDP 131 and 60), and the picker says so while Claude isn't set up. A niche with two formats may make more than two of one a day, just enough to fill the plan's day (`chooseFormats` in `lib/shorts/learn.ts`).
- **Record-book facts** (`lib/server/shorts/fresh.ts`): the sports, cars and gaming niches may name real clubs, teams, players, drivers, car makers and games, only for facts in the record books, under their own rule: winners and results of finished seasons, finals and races, nothing current or still running, no records that can still be broken, and no fees, salaries or prices. The second call that checks every question still drops anything that could change. Each niche's topics come with an era picked at random, so its channels don't all ask the same things. Gaming leaves out games made for young children, Money never gives advice about saving, investing or earning, and Cars never mentions crashes. A choice whose two sides both name a country, a people, a faith or who people love is refused, since that sets people against each other; a place on one side only is fine (`pairProblem` in `lib/shorts/checks.ts`).
- **Each channel keeps its own note** (`channels.shorts_note`): why a channel's Shorts fell short is kept on that channel, since one channel can run dry while another never does, and the notification says it once for each channel.
- **Make one like this, by niche** (`makeLike` in `app/app/(shell)/trends/actions.ts`): Trends makes the Short for the channel with the most room whose niche makes that format, or that hasn't picked one. When none does, or Claude didn't answer, or a Ball League channel's matches are still waiting for a swipe, Trends says so instead of making something else.
- **A LIVE round goes to a channel without a niche** (`make` in `lib/server/shorts/generate.ts`): a round from the creator's LIVE becomes a Short only for the first channel that gets Shorts and hasn't picked what it's about, since a niche's Shorts are only about it. With every channel on a niche, rounds wait their three days and go.
- **The Ball League** (`lib/battle/league.ts`, `lib/server/shorts/league.ts`): a channel in it gets a cast of eight named balls, one of each power, kept for good, playing seasons: a round robin of 28 matches, then 1st against 4th and 2nd against 3rd in the semi-finals, then a final, first to two wins. That's 31 Shorts a season, one match each. A win is 3 points and a draw 1; ties go by wins, then the match between the two, then an order drawn when the season began. Ball names come from two lists of over 100 words, with no colours, peoples, places, faiths or cute words, checked against the off-limits list, and are claimed in the content ledger, so no two leagues share one while new ones are left.
- **One real fight, never picked**: each game of a match is the next usable seed of the ones drawn for it when its season began, and no fight is run twice; only a knockout's drawn game is played again, as a new fight. The drama pick is never used, since keeping the most dramatic of 40 runs moves who wins by 15 points or more in 19 of 28 matchups. Seeds stay with the server: signed-in users can read every column of a match but its seeds, so nobody can know a result before it's played (`supabase/migrations/20261018040000_niches_league.sql`). A seed is skipped only when another account already has that exact fight, checked before it runs; one this account has is its own, so a retry after a save that went wrong plays the same fight and gets the same result (`claimFight` in `lib/server/shorts/ledger.ts`). When another account has every seed a match has, a new one is drawn and kept with the match before it runs.
- **League health** (`LEAGUE_HEALTH` in `lib/battle/league.ts`): in the league each power starts with its own health, Spike 20, Fibonacci 4, Grow 17, Sword 20, Heal 27, Speed 18, Clone 21 and Bomb 27, set so every power wins titles (about 7% to 18% each in simulated seasons, with a few matchups still near certain). A season keeps the table it began with, so a change only affects seasons to come. `pnpm league-balance` plays seasons to check it.
- **A match counts once it's played** (`makeLeague` in `lib/server/shorts/generate.ts`): its result is saved when it's played, posted or not, swiped away or not, and the table is only those results. Only as many new matches are played as fill a day's worth (the plan's posts a day) waiting for a swipe, so a season never runs on without the creator, and a skipped match is always replaced by the next one while the day's replacements last; the swipe card only says so while that's true. A league never runs dry.
- **What a league Short says** (`remotion/Battle.tsx`): the two balls in their own colours, each with its power, its place and its won, drawn and lost; which match it is; what happened before over the first three seconds (a streak, its last result, or how it finished a season played to the end); then what's at stake, only when the rules make it certain; and after the result, what it means (top of the table, in the top four for good, an upset of at least three places and three points, the champion). All of it comes from results and the rules, so it stays true whatever happens after, and nothing teases a match to come. A league Short never loops, and asks "Did you call it?" only where comments are on, as every battle now does.
- **A knockout's games in one Short** (`lib/battle/series.ts`): a semi-final's drawn game is played again with the next seed, and after four draws the higher-placed ball goes through. The final is one Short whose games play in turn, with a gap after each that says how it went and how the final stands. One too long for 175 seconds plays its fights faster, only as much as it needs and at most twice as fast, and says so on screen; past that, drawn games after the first show as a short card, and every game that decided something plays in full. The league page lists every game.
- **Matches post in order** (`approve` and `retimeLate` in `lib/server/shorts/queue.ts`): a match kept while an earlier one of its league is still to post gets a time at least an hour after it, on YouTube and on TikTok, and matches given new times after a reconnect keep their order. An earlier match kept later can't be put first, so the later video shows its result first: a spoiler, never untrue. For Shorts the creator posts themselves, the queue says which match to post first.
- **Changing niche stops the season** (`stopIfLeft` in `lib/server/shorts/league.ts`): its results stay, no champion is crowned and its unplayed seeds are never used. Coming back starts a new season with the same eight balls, and a stopped season is never quoted as last season. A channel that's disconnected, switched off or past the plan's room gets no new Shorts, so its season waits where it is.
- **The league page** (`app/app/(shell)/shorts/league/page.tsx`): a League tab shows while a channel is in the Ball League, with where its season is, its table, every match with its seeds once played and where its Short is, and the season's health table. Once a season is over, it also gives each season's champion and how the final went, or how far a stopped season got, and each ball's record over every match it has played in every season, stopped ones too, with its titles (`allTime` in `lib/shorts/league.ts`). Those only show then, since until a season ends they'd say what the table says. It opens on a league that gets Shorts now, and the account's own Shorts' league, while it has no channel, has its own chip. The queue gives the season in a line, read from that season alone, and says when new matches are waiting for a swipe.

## Trends

- **YouTube only**, through the official Data API. TikTok doesn't offer trending data to apps like this (its Research API is for academic researchers), so there's no TikTok trends.
- **Thirteen niches** (`lib/trends/niches.ts`), six of them one per Shorts format so "make one like this" can make one; the other seven (satisfying, facts, gaming, football, Formula 1, NBA and cars) are there to watch, the last three for channels about them. People pick which to see. With none picked, Trends shows the ones their channels are about, or that their own Shorts are about while they have no channel, outlined among the chips (`channelTrendNiches` in `lib/server/niches.ts`); with nothing picked there either, it shows every niche.
- **Rising means growing fast**: views per hour since upload. Each niche is searched once a day for Shorts (three minutes or less) uploaded in the last two days, and the 25 growing fastest are kept for a week; Rising now shows the last three days. That's 13 of YouTube's 100 searches a day and about 13 units (`refreshTrends` in `lib/server/trends.ts`). It was every four hours (60 searches); once a day still catches what's rising, and every card says when it was measured. Each niche is claimed in `trend_checks` before it's searched, so two checkers at once never both search it, and a run stops at the first niche it can't check, since the rest would go the same way: they wait for the reset when Trends' quota is used up, or an hour when YouTube doesn't answer. The page says when the niches were last checked, and names any shown that haven't been in over a day, with why.
- **Only YouTube's API, never its feeds**: a channel's feed on youtube.com costs no quota, but the Shorts-only one is undocumented and could change without notice, a feed only lists about the latest 15 uploads, and it only finds channels on a list someone keeps by hand. A server reading youtube.com outside the API is also automated access, right before YouTube's audit, and the public Trends page promises nothing scraped. Since June 2026 searches have an allowance of their own, so they cost posting nothing anyway.
- **Made for kids, by niche** (`kidsShare` in `lib/server/trends.ts`): the look at what a search found also asks for each Short's status, and keeps YouTube's own made-for-kids verdict. The page shows, for each niche shown, how many of the last three days' rising Shorts YouTube marked made for kids, always with how many that's of, and leaves out the ones YouTube said nothing about. It's YouTube's word on other people's public videos and suggests nothing: whether a creator's own channel or Short is made for kids stays their call.
- **Best times** come from the channel's own Shorts: each one's plays at 24 hours against the channel's usual, by day of the week and three-hour block in its own timezone (`postingTimes` in `lib/server/trends.ts`, `hourResults` in `lib/server/shorts/hours.ts`). They stay on plays, which are read at an exact hour, when first days go by engaged views: a Short's engaged share says nothing about when people watch, most first days would carry the channel's usual one, and YouTube Analytics reports whole Pacific days, which would tilt the hours toward early ones. YouTube doesn't share when a channel's viewers are online through its API.
- **Formats right now** is the middle growth rate of the rising Shorts in each format over three days, next to how that format does on the channel.
- **"Make one like this"** makes a Short of the same format straight into the queue, and counts toward the plan's Shorts.
- **The morning run follows the trends** (`trendLean` and `withTrends` in `lib/shorts/learn.ts`, `formatGrowth` in `lib/server/trends.ts`): after a channel's own results set the day's odds, each format it makes leans toward how fast a typical rising Short in that format is growing on YouTube Shorts over the last three days, against the middle of its formats. The lean is square-rooted and kept between 0.8 and 1.5, so a trend tilts the day without taking it over, and the fifth-of-the-best floor still holds. A format with fewer than 3 rising Shorts in the window, or a channel with only one rated format, isn't leaned at all. When a format leans 1.15 or more, its Shorts' "why" says it's rising on YouTube Shorts right now. "Make one like this" doesn't lean: it already is the trend.

## YouTube quota

- **One project, counted by Hookline** (`lib/shorts/quota.ts`, `lib/server/quota.ts`): YouTube's quota belongs to Hookline's one Google Cloud project, so every account shares it. Since June 2026 a project gets three allowances a day, reset at midnight Pacific: 100 uploads, 100 searches and 10,000 units for every other Data API call. Every call is counted in `youtube_quota` before it's made, by its quota day, its allowance and what it's for, one at a time for each allowance under a lock, so calls at once can't go past it. A call that fails still counts, as it does at Google. The quota day runs from 00:05 Pacific, five minutes after Google's reset, so a call or a refusal in those minutes belongs to the day before and can never close a new day. `YOUTUBE_DAILY_UNITS`, `YOUTUBE_DAILY_UPLOADS` and `YOUTUBE_DAILY_SEARCHES` replace Google's defaults once it grants more; anything but a whole number of 1 or more uses the default, so a typo can't stop a kind of call while the app says it comes back at the reset.
- **Posting comes first** (`SHARES` in `lib/shorts/quota.ts`): of the units, Trends may use 5%, views 15%, link checks 30%, connecting channels 5% and YouTube Live 25%. Posting's checks have no share of their own, so at least 20% (2,000 of 10,000) is always left for them. YouTube Analytics is another API with its own count, and no limit until `YOUTUBE_DAILY_ANALYTICS` is set. A call with no room left isn't made: whatever asked waits for the reset and says so (a Short in the queue, a pasted link, connecting a channel in Settings or while getting started), instead of failing at YouTube.
- **When YouTube says it's used up anyway** (`failCounted` in `lib/server/youtube.ts`), that allowance is closed for everything until the reset, whatever Hookline counted, so nothing more is asked that day. YouTube doesn't document its reason for the June 2026 allowances, so any reason naming quota counts, and so does dailyLimitExceeded. If YouTube ever gives one that doesn't name quota, the Short is marked failed with YouTube's own message instead, and the creator can try it again.
- **Each plan's uploads a day** (`uploadCap` in `lib/billing/plans.ts`): Pro uploads up to 6 Shorts a day and Creator 10 for each connected YouTube channel, twice a day's Shorts, so a day held back can catch up and no account takes everyone's. The server counts them in `youtube_account_days`, never from the account's own Shorts, which people can change. The day's uploads go to Shorts due before the reset first, then to the accounts with the fewest so far, then to the soonest, and an account at its plan's uploads is left out of the line, however many it has ready. A public Short goes up at most a day before its time.
- **Failures back off** (`postDue` in `lib/server/shorts/post.ts`): an upload that fails for a reason that passes is tried again after half an hour, since every try counts against everyone's 100. When YouTube fails two uploads in a row with an error of its own, or can't be reached, every Short in line waits that half hour too, since each would fail the same way and still count, so an outage costs two tries each half hour instead of the day's uploads. One such failure alone stays that Short's own, like one YouTube asks to slow down or whose channel's access is refused, so a Short YouTube keeps failing can't hold up everyone else's. The checks that an upload went up drop to every half hour after its first half hour, failed ones too, so one YouTube is slow with, or won't answer for, can't use hundreds of units a day.
- **50 link checks per account a day** (`LINK_CHECKS_A_DAY` in `lib/shorts/quota.ts`): more than any plan makes, so it only stops one account using everyone's checks. A link takes one however many of the account's channels are asked. One that can't be checked today isn't marked posted, and leaving the link out still works.
- **Never a second project**: YouTube's rules allow one API project per app and forbid making more to get quota, and Google can take the key away for it. More quota only comes through YouTube's audit and quota extension form, which a parent or guardian sends (NEXT.md, section 8). The YouTube quota card on `/admin` shows each day's use as Hookline counted it, for that form.
- **If Google changes its costs again**: costs live in one table (`METHODS`) and limits in the environment. If the project turns out to be on the old single 10,000 units, `METHODS` (uploads and searches into the units at Google's costs), `DEFAULT_LIMITS` (units only) and Trends' share (about 14% for 13 searches a day) change together. Until then a refusal from Google still closes the day, and Shorts wait with the same words.

## Earnings

- **Real figures only, from where they are**: YouTube Analytics (daily views, engaged views, new subscribers and, for channels that can read it, YouTube's estimated revenue, refreshed for the last 35 days every 6 hours, each report counted in the quota ledger through `analyticsReport` in `lib/server/youtube-analytics.ts`, and a refusal of engaged views or revenue dropping only that measure, so views and subscribers always sync, while engaged views added up across channels, and a goal on them, name any channel YouTube hasn't given them for, since their total can't count it); TikTok's view counts (it only gives each video's total, so a day's views are the rise since the last reading, and a day without a reading shows as unknown, not zero); the gift coins seen on the streamer's own verified LIVEs, labelled as what viewers sent as the game counted it, never as money, with unverified LIVEs shown apart and not counted; and Stripe sales for people who sell something. YouTube's and TikTok's figures are each channel's own (`lib/server/earnings.ts`). With more than one YouTube channel, the page shows them added up or one at a time (`?channel=`), each card named for its channel, and all of them on the way back from connecting one, which nobody picked (`pickedChannel` in `lib/shorts/proof.ts`); TikTok, the gift coins seen and Stripe are the account's as a whole, so they show with all of them. A disconnected channel's are deleted with the access they were read with, and connecting it again reads the last 35 days back.
- **Stripe** needs a restricted key (`rk_live_…`) that can read balance transactions; a secret key is refused. It's stored encrypted. Sales are charges and payments less refunds, before Stripe's fees, kept per currency.
- **YouTube runs 2 to 3 days behind**: days after the last one YouTube has reported, and before its first, are "not reported yet" or unknown, never 0 views or $0; a day skipped between reported days is 0. Each YouTube card says how far it's reported up to. Several channels' figures are added up just as carefully (`addUp` in `lib/earnings/series.ts`): a day shows once every channel Hookline was reading then has reported it. A channel counts from its first report, so one added later doesn't make the days before it unknown, and one that needs reconnecting counts up to its last report, so it doesn't hold the rest back. The page says so, and names any channel whose figures start later or stop.
- **Stripe is read back 35 days** from when it's connected, so days before that are unknown, not $0, and the card says where its figures start.
- **Super Chats and Super Stickers** (`syncSuperChats` in `lib/server/earnings.ts`, `lib/earnings/superchats.ts`): while YouTube Live is on, from YouTube's own list, only for the channels of people who said they have Super Chat, so the many that can't get any cost nothing; every 6 hours, by local day and currency, labelled as what viewers paid before YouTube's share, and never credited to the game. YouTube lists 30 days, so each channel's figures start at the first whole day Hookline read, and a day is known only between that and its last read: a read that stops at its 40 pages starts the day after the oldest it reached, one that fails changes nothing, and one that doesn't join on to the last starts afresh, so a day nobody read never shows as none. Each channel's are its own, added up across channels like YouTube's other figures, and deleted with them when it's disconnected.
- **Charts**: one measure per chart, side by side rather than two on one axis, as daily bars from zero, with each day's exact figure on hover and in a table for screen readers.
- **Goals** are set on any of those measures, for this month (in the account's timezone) or all time, and add up every channel, so they're set with all of them shown.

## YouTube Analytics and the Partner Program

- **Plays and engaged views** (`lib/shorts/proof.ts`): since March 2025 a Shorts view on YouTube counts every start or replay, so Hookline calls that count plays, and says it's what YouTube now shows as views. Engaged views leave out the plays swiped past, and are what YouTube counts for the Partner Program. Only YouTube's own figures show as numbers, each with the day YouTube has reported up to. Hookline's comparisons show only as "1.8× usual", never under a heading that says YouTube: a Short's page gives its first day against the usual one a row of its own headed "Hookline's comparison", apart from its plays from YouTube and YouTube Analytics' figures (`components/shorts/vs-usual.tsx`), and the line under What's working's bars says the comparisons are Hookline's, whatever they went by. No figure Hookline worked out is ever shown under one of YouTube's names: YouTube's API rules don't allow metrics that replace its own until Hookline is audited and approved for them (NEXT.md, section 8).
- **Each Short's YouTube Analytics** (`syncAnalytics` in `lib/server/shorts/proof.ts`): once a day each connected YouTube channel is read with its own access, and only for the Shorts Hookline knows are on it, 200 to a query, never one with no channel, since its id could be anyone's video. It keeps each Short's plays, engaged views, average watch time and percentage (which passes 100% when people watch again), and YouTube's revenue estimate where YouTube estimates revenue for the channel. A refusal of revenue means it can't be read, never $0. Every report counts in the quota ledger under analytics; a limit stops the run and that channel goes first after the reset. Nothing is kept from a read that fails part way, and two runs never read one channel twice. /admin only counts the channels read in the last day and those that can't be read, and why, never their figures (`analyticsHealth`).
- **Kept only while Hookline can read it** (`forgetLapsed` in `lib/server/shorts/proof.ts`): YouTube's rules let Hookline keep these figures only while it keeps reading them, so every Short still kept is read again within 20 days, a Short's figures go 30 days after its last read and a channel's 30 days after its last full read, and everything goes at once when the channel is disconnected (a trigger on `channels`) or the account is deleted. The channel's reason it couldn't be read stays, so its card can say why. Earnings' daily figures and the learning loop's readings came before this and aren't covered yet (NEXT.md, section 8).
- **First days by engaged views** (`firstDayLift` in `lib/shorts/learn.ts`, `learnDue` in `lib/server/shorts/stats.ts`): a Short's engaged share is its engaged views over its plays from the first reading that covers the day it went up and the day after, in Pacific time, set once. Only a reading that runs no more than 6 days past the day it went up gives one, since YouTube's totals run from the day a Short went up: a Short first read later, like every Short already up when Hookline started reading YouTube Analytics, gets none, so a share over the rest of its life never counts as a first day's. Its first day is its plays at 24 hours times that share, against the channel's usual first day times the middle of its Shorts' shares, once 3 have one. A Short without its own share, or a channel without a usual one, goes by plays exactly as before: nothing stands in for a missing share, so a comparison never mixes the two. A first day waits for its share, typically 3 to 5 days, goes by plays after 6 days, and goes by plays at once when no share can come (no channel, one that isn't connected or that Hookline can't read, Google not set up, or reads failing for 2 days). It assumes a Short's first-day plays were engaged at the rate of its first two Pacific days, since YouTube Analytics only reports whole days, a few days late.
- **Each first day learned once** (`learned_at` on `video_stats`): each is claimed before it's learned, so two runners (two workers, or the worker and `/api/cron/stats`) never learn the same one twice, as they could before, and an account's first days take turns under a lock, so no result is lost. Readings from before, and any that code from before writes during a deploy, count as learned, on plays, through the columns' defaults; a later migration drops them once no old worker can run. Only collectStats learns, on every run.
- **Who's watching** (`components/app/audience.tsx`): each channel's signed-in viewers 13 and over, by age group and gender, over 90 days, as YouTube's percentages. It says nobody under 13 is in it, so it can't tell anyone whether kids watch, and it sits next to the channel's made-for-kids answers: the creator's, YouTube's verdict from when the channel was connected (it isn't read again, since it drives the comments-off re-renders), and its posted Shorts' answers and YouTube's verdicts on them. Nothing suggests an answer. YouTube shares nothing for small channels, and the card says so.
- **Partner Program progress** (`lib/earnings/ypp.ts`, `components/app/partner-meter.tsx`): every bar is in one config, copied from YouTube's own pages with the page and the day someone checked it. Only today's bars ship: 500 subscribers, 3 public uploads in 90 days and 3 million Shorts views in 90 days or 3,000 watch hours in 12 months for fan funding and Shopping; 1,000 subscribers and 10 million or 4,000 for ad revenue. They're checked against YouTube's page before release, and a later set, like the one reported for February 2027, goes in only with what YouTube's own page says (NEXT.md, section 8). Once one is in, the meter says what changes and from which date, built from that set's own numbers (`notesFor`), with a link to YouTube's page on it. Each bar says what needs someone 18 or older: AdSense for ad revenue, and memberships, Super Chat and Super Thanks for fan funding. The figures are YouTube Analytics' engaged views on Shorts and watch hours on long videos, and the public subscriber count (rounded down by YouTube), not YouTube's own count, so a channel is only ever "past these bars by YouTube Analytics", never reached or qualified, with YouTube Studio's Earn page for the real answer and a line saying YouTube reviews every channel. A figure Hookline doesn't know is "Not known" and never past, uploads count only those Hookline knows of, and big figures round down. A channel YouTube estimates ad revenue for is told it already shares in it; revenue from fans alone isn't ad revenue, so its bars stay. No countdown, no pace and no projection.
- **Connect again** (`connectAgain` in `lib/shorts/proof.ts`): a channel Hookline isn't allowed to read, or lost access to, links straight to Google's screen for that channel, which updates the same channel, and comes back to Earnings. One connected again since its last try is read at the next run rather than a day later, and its old reason stops showing.
- **Typical account** (`cohorts` in `lib/server/cohorts.ts`, `components/app/typical-account.tsx`): /admin shows each of the last 12 whole weeks' sign-ups, after all of them together, with the middle account's figures rather than the best one's: how many have YouTube connected now, kept a Short, posted one YouTube confirmed in their first 4 weeks, how many Shorts that was (the middle account's count, and the lowest and highest of the middle half), sent one to TikTok, and still said yes in week 4. Each middle figure is one an account really had. It's built only from Hookline's own records, never YouTube's numbers, since YouTube's API rules don't allow combining different people's channels, so it's also the only typical figure a public page could ever carry. Posted counts only Shorts YouTube confirmed, which only the server can mark, and TikTok only what was sent there, not drafts. The first 4 weeks' figures count only accounts at least 4 weeks old and say Not yet until 5 of them are, and a week with fewer than 5 accounts shows no figures, so no figure is one or two people's.

## Making a battle the same fight everywhere

- Browsers don't agree on `Math.sin` and `Math.cos` to the last digit (Chrome changed its implementation after the version in Node 22), and a physics simulation turns that into a different fight: about a third of test fights played out differently in Chrome than in Node. So the battle simulation uses its own `sin`, `cos` and `hypot` (`lib/battle/trig.ts`), ports of fdlibm, which is what Node 22 uses. They match Node bit for bit (checked on 3.3 million inputs), so every fight is the same one it always was, and now the browser preview, the renderer and the server all compute the identical fight. `tests/trig.test.ts` pins the exact bits, and `tests/battle.test.ts` checks that every matchup still makes exactly the same fights.

## The platform around the sections

- **Notifications only for things that happened**: a Short going live on YouTube or out to TikTok, one that couldn't be made or was turned down, nothing new left to make, one ready to download (when nothing will post it), the morning's Shorts, a Short whose first day beat the usual by half again or more (worse results only steer what's made, they don't notify), a LIVE's recap once it's been quiet for 20 minutes, plan changes, and accounts needing reconnecting. Each event has a key, so it notifies once however many jobs notice it. Opening the bell counts them as seen. There are no emails yet; that needs an email service and is in NEXT.md's "Later".
- **Ctrl K (⌘K on a Mac)** opens a palette to go anywhere or do the common things (connect an account, switch the theme, sign out), matching any word typed against each command's name and the words people use for it.
- **Toasts** say what just happened after an action that doesn't change the page much: a swipe ("Posting Tue 4:00 PM" only when something will post it, otherwise "Kept. Best time to post it: Tue 4:00 PM"), a saved title, TikTok choices.
- **Nothing says a Short posts unless something will post it**: on Free, and before YouTube's audit except on test channels, the swipe card, the queue, the calendar, each Short's history and its "why" call its time the best time to post it, and Home's setup list and the note on going Pro don't promise posting.
- **The calendar** shows each Short on the local day and hour it posts or posted, in the account's own timezone, Sunday first. On a phone it's a list of days instead of a grid.
- **The library** shows every Short said yes to, newest first, 24 at a time, with YouTube Analytics' engaged views and plays and the day YouTube reported them up to once it's been read, plays at the latest checkpoint until then, and the first day against the usual Short, by the learning loop's own measure. The usual is the middle of the last 30 first days read on time, so it's plays a first day really had, and it's shown in plays, never as engaged views Hookline worked out; a first day read late is scaled back the way the learning loop does it and says "about". A Short's page labels each reading with how long after posting it was really taken, says what its first day was compared by and that the comparison is Hookline's, and shows YouTube Analytics' own figures for it with the day they run up to.
- **What's working** draws each format's first-day result against the usual as one bar from a middle line, on a scale where twice and half are the same distance away, up to four times either way. A kind of Short (a power, a subject, a tone) is only called out after 3 results and when it's at least 15% away from usual. A line under the bars says what the first days were compared by (engaged views, engaged views where YouTube has reported them and plays before that, or plays), and that the comparisons are Hookline's (`measureLine` in `lib/shorts/insights.ts`).
- **Each Short's page** lists only what happened or is booked to, from the times saved as it happens (made, approved, rendered, uploaded, public, TikTok); nothing is inferred.
- **The worker checks in** every 30 seconds; one silent for 2 minutes counts as stopped. People only hear about it when their own Shorts are waiting to be made.
- **The admin page** (`/admin`) is for the emails in `ADMIN_EMAILS`; anyone else gets the ordinary not-found page. Revenue there is at list price: active plans and ones whose card Stripe is retrying, yearly plans as a twelfth, trials not counted. Stripe has the figures after fees and discounts.
- **On a phone's home screen**, Hookline opens straight into the app, in its own window.
